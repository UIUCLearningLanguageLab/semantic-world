## The split of stage a5 (decided by Jon, October 8, 2026)

Stage a5 is built in two halves, so that every test passes at the end of each half:

- **a5a** (this stage): the corpus moves onto the world package. It reads world runs, uses `semantic_world.world.episodes` for its scenes, and takes the new labels, the quantifiers (CG.59 to CG.62), aspect (CG.64), `able` and `legal` in event groundings, and the default of "A principle for defaults" for the content of category documents. The taxonomy does not change.
- **a5b** (a later session): the taxonomy takes the new labels and loses CAN features, verbs, and exposure; the world package drops its label translation; the remaining defaults change (event-type density and feature base rates); the fixtures are regenerated.

### How the pieces stand today

The corpus loads a `TaxonomyResult` (old labels) and reads it everywhere: the lexicon's concepts come from the tree, the features, the verb tree, and the exposed projections; `Truth` judges class-level facts by the fixed test over the taxonomy's rules and categories, relation facts by `result.relations.matrix`, and events against `Scene` objects from `corpus/scenes.py`; `Facts` builds rule statements from the taxonomy's rules; the planner, the mentions, the statistics, and the word-form request read the tree, the instances, the vectors, and `categories_generative.csv`. Label prefixes (`IS.`, `HAS.`, `CAN.`, `SC.`, `V`, `C`, `I`, `SN.`, `L.`, `D.`, `R.`, `X.`) are parsed in `propositions.py`, `grammar.py`, `mentions.py`, `interpret.py`, `readings.py`, `renderings.py`, `testsets.py`, and the tests. The quantifiers are `all`, `most`, `some`, `no`, and `generic`, with the generic's meaning a setting, and every event carries an aspect drawn by the scene generator.

The world package gives, in memory, `WorldResult.definition.runtime()` (entities by index, base and derived fluents, event types with roles, preconditions, and effects, the rule-set identity), `able_table` (every requirement over all entities and all ordered pairs, categories of event types included), `EpisodeGenerator.episode(rng, label, seed)` with `Relatedness.from_taxonomy`, `History` with `replay`, and `WorldResult.taxonomy`, the embedded taxonomy run with old labels.

### Plan for the stage

1. **The world input.** `corpus/config.py` reads `world: {config, seed}` or `world: {run}` and rejects `taxonomy` with an error that names `world`. `corpus/world.py` becomes the one boundary between the two vocabularies: it defines the world (`define` with the statistics episodes skipped), checks a run folder against the regenerated world (the rule-set identity, `entities.csv`, and the derived manifest), and builds a `World` object that gives every other corpus module what it needs in the new labels: the categories with their levels, parents, and paths; the entities with their leaves; the PROPERTY and PART features with their values; the scalars and poles; the event types with their tree; the able tables; the patient capacities; the relatedness and the leaf similarity; the fixed test and the rule terms (wrapped over the taxonomy's machinery with the labels translated at the call); and the meanings table for the word-form request. Stage a5b removes the translation from this one module.
2. **Scenes.** `corpus/scenes.py` is deleted. The planner calls `EpisodeGenerator.episode` with the scene's part of `corpus:scenes`, its own seed instance, and the label `SCENE.<n>`. A small module `corpus/histories.py` gives the corpus's view of a `History`: the events in time order with their step and scene, what happened, and what involves an entity. `scenes.jsonl` holds histories. The scene keys `event_type_weights`, `policy` (default `uniform_event`), and `initial` join the corpus configuration; `verb_weights` gets an error.
3. **Labels.** Every label the corpus writes follows "Labels": the world's labels come from the world package, and the corpus's own become `LEXEME.<n>`, `DOC.<n>`, `DOC.<n>.SENT.<k>`, `PROP.<n>`, `REF.<n>`, `VAR.<n>`, and `SCENE.<n>.EVENTINSTANCE.<k>`. Every place that parses a prefix is updated. Word-form labels stay (stage a6).
4. **Lexicon.** The concept types become `category`, `is`, `has`, `state`, `event_unary`, `event`, `event_category`, `patient_projection`, and `scalar`, with `named_proportion.patient_projection: 0.25`; the patient capacities are all two-place leaf event types, no longer the taxonomy's exposed ones.
5. **Quantifiers.** `NEC_ALL`, `ALL`, `MOST`, `SOME`, `NO`, `NEC_NO`; no `GEN`; the language settings `universal_words`, `most.usage_min`, and `bare_plural.expresses`; the bare plural as a surface choice that records the real quantifier, with quantifier readings; the law-like sets redefined; the propositional rendering with `NEC(ALL(...))` and `NEC(NO(...))`.
6. **Aspect.** Events carry no aspect; each report draws its aspect from the document's part of `corpus:mentions`, with `documents.one_aspect_per_event`; test items draw theirs from a part of `corpus:grammar`.
7. **Event groundings.** `able` and `legal` replace `possible`; the test sets keep the split into possible (able) and impossible.
8. **Category documents.** `documents.content_kind_weights: proportional | equal`, default `proportional`.
9. **Configuration changes.** Every key of "Configuration changes" that belongs to this stage, with an error for each old key that names the new key.
10. **The world package.** `define(config, episode_stats=False)` for in-memory use; `world:episodes` in the stream table; the a2 and a4 tests updated.
11. **Tests.** Every corpus test updated; the truth oracle rewritten over the world run's files, with `able` and `legal` through the brute-force evaluator; the participants test against a reference of the old draw; the replay test; the law-like test; the old-key errors; the end-to-end runs of the tiny and default corpora, and the tiny corpus rendered with word forms.
12. **Documents.** This file, `docs/DECISIONS.md`, `docs/guides/CORPUS.md` (with every quoted figure re-checked against the regenerated tiny corpus), and `CLAUDE.md`.

## Engineering choices made while building

Numbered for `docs/DECISIONS.md` (WM.E71 and following). Each is Claude Code's choice inside the stage's scope; none changes a file format that the specification decides.

1. **`define` without the statistics episodes.** `semantic_world.world.generate.define(config, *, episode_stats=True)` takes a keyword that skips the 1,000 statistics episodes for in-memory use. `WorldResult.stats["episodes"]` is then None, and `write_result` runs the episodes before it writes `world_stats.yaml`, so a written run always has them. The corpus defines its world with `episode_stats=False`.
2. **The world's `episodes` stream is recorded.** `world:episodes` joins the stream table of the world package (`STREAM_NAMES`), so `config.yaml` of a world run lists it under `provenance.streams` (Jon's third ruling on stage a4). The a2 provenance test and the a4 episode tests were updated.
3. **One translation module.** `corpus/world.py` is the only corpus module that reads the old labels. `load_world(config)` defines the world from a configuration file, or regenerates a run folder in memory from its `config.yaml`, and builds a `World` view: categories with their levels, parents, children, paths, and leaves; entities with their leaves; PROPERTY and PART features with their values and kinds; scalars and poles; event types with their tree, arity, and category flag; the able tables of the runtime; the patient capacities; the relatedness and the leaf similarity; the fixed test and the rule terms of the taxonomy's rules; the initial rates; and the meanings table of the word-form request. Every label it gives is translated once, at the boundary; the other modules never call `labels.translate`. Stage a5b removes the translation from this module alone.
4. **A run folder is checked, not trusted.** With `world: {run: FOLDER}`, the corpus regenerates the world from the folder's `config.yaml` and compares the rule-set identity, the bytes of `entities.csv`, and the derived manifest (`derived/manifest.yaml`) with the folder. A difference is a `CorpusError` that names what differs. `config.yaml` itself is not compared (it records the git commit), and extra files are ignored.
5. **The world's identity in the provenance.** `config.yaml` of a corpus run records `provenance.world`: `source` (`config` or `run`), `path`, `name`, `seed`, `config_hash` (the hash of the resolved world configuration), and `rule_set_id`. The key `taxonomy` is gone. The corpus seed stays independent of the world's seed.
6. **Scenes through the world package.** `corpus/scenes.py` is deleted. `corpus/histories.py` wraps `EpisodeGenerator` as `SceneGenerator`: the planner draws each scene's seed instance as before, and calls `episode(rng, label, seed)` with the scene's part of the corpus's `scenes` stream and the label `SCENE.<n>`. The module gives the corpus's view of a `History`: `SceneEvent` (label, scene, step, type, agent, patient), `scene_events` in time order, `events_at`, `involving`, and `happened_in`. Event labels are `SCENE.<n>.EVENTINSTANCE.<k>`, numbered across the whole history in time order. `scenes.jsonl` holds `History.to_json()` records.
7. **Scene settings.** `scene.event_type_weights` (checked against the world's performable event types: an event-type category or any other label is a `ConfigError` naming `scene.event_type_weights.<label>`), `scene.policy` (any policy of `semantic_world.world.policies`, default `uniform_event`), and `scene.initial` (`keep` or `redraw`) join the configuration. `scene.verb_weights` is an error that names `scene.event_type_weights`. The other scene keys keep their meaning, and are passed to `EpisodeSettings`.
8. **The corpus's labels.** `LEXEME.<n>`, `DOC.<n>`, `DOC.<n>.SENT.<k>`, `PROP.<n>`, `REF.<n>`, and `VAR.<n>`, from the constants in `corpus/lexicon.py`, `corpus/planner.py`, `corpus/mentions.py`, and `corpus/renderings.py`. The notation of the test-set format check takes the indices out of a label and keeps its prefix (`CATEGORY.#`, `SCENE.#.EVENTINSTANCE.#`). Word-form labels do not change (stage a6).
9. **Branch markers read the new labels.** `wordforms.assign.branch_of` takes the branch of a meaning from the numbers after a prefix (`CATEGORY.1.2.3` is in `CATEGORY.1` at depth 1), and keeps the old form (`C1.2.3` in `C1`), so the word-form pipeline's own tests and fixtures are unchanged. Nothing else in the word-form pipeline parses a label.
10. **Lexicon concept types.** `category`, `is`, `has`, `state`, `event_unary`, `event`, `event_category`, `patient_projection`, and `scalar`, with `named_proportion.patient_projection: 0.25` and every other default 1. `state` names nothing until stage a7, and its count in `stats.yaml` is 0. The old keys `can`, `verb`, and `verb_category` are errors that name the new keys.
11. **Patient capacities.** The adjectives of patient capacities are `CANBE.<two-place leaf event type>` for every two-place leaf event type, no longer the taxonomy's exposed projections. An event-type category has no patient capacity. A two-place event type that is able for every ordered pair, or for none, gets no word, as before; `stats.yaml` lists it under `lexicon.event_types_without_word`.
12. **The quantifiers.** `nec_all`, `all`, `most`, `some`, `no`, and `nec_no`; `generic` is gone. `quantifiers.universal_words` (`nec`, `extensional`, `either`) says which quantifiers the words "all" and "no" state; `quantifiers.most.usage_min` (default 0.7) is the share below which a true `most` is not stated; `quantifiers.some.exclude_all` (default true) keeps `some` from being stated when the language's universal is true; `quantifiers.bare_plural.expresses` (default `[most]`) lists what a bare plural states, and a bare plural also states `nec_all` and `nec_no` for membership facts and rule statements, and the negative counterpart of each listed quantifier for a negative fact. `quantifiers.generic_rate` is the chance of a bare plural when a word could state the fact as well. The old keys `all_grounding`, `most.min_proportion`, and `generic` are errors that name the new keys. The weights take the keys `nec_all`, `all`, `most`, `some`, `none`, and `nec_none`.
13. **The strongest statable fact.** `Facts.class_fact` returns the strongest true proposition that the language can state (with a word or with a bare plural), trying the quantifiers strongest first: `nec_all`, `all`, `most`, `some` for a positive fact, and `nec_no`, `no`, `most ... not`, `some ... not` for a negative one. A document states that quantifier; the sentence's `strength` is the same quantifier. With `universal_words: nec`, an extensional `all` is not statable, so a fact that holds of every instance without a rule falls to `most` (or to `some`).
14. **The bare plural is a surface choice.** The plan of a class-level sentence carries its quantifier (`SentencePlan.quantifier`), and its subject's determiner is the word that states it, or none for a bare plural. The logical form, the propositional rendering, and the test items always carry the real quantifier. `interpret` takes the quantifier as an argument, since the words do not give it. A sentence's `readings` list its level reading and then the quantifiers that its words allow: a bare plural allows `nec_all` and the quantifiers of `bare_plural.expresses`; "all" and "no" allow what `universal_words` says; "most" and "some" allow themselves. `stats.yaml` counts the sets of quantifier readings under `ambiguity.quantifier_readings`, apart from the level ambiguity.
15. **Where `nec` applies.** The `nec` quantifiers apply to PROPERTY, PART, and one-place event-type facts (the fixed test over the rules) and to membership (true when the category is an ancestor). A relation fact, a patient-capacity fact, and a fact whose subject has a relative clause are invalid with a `nec` quantifier, and take the extensional `all` and `no`. A rule statement is `nec_all`.
16. **The class-level scalar pole has no quantifier.** "Penguins are big" is a statement about the category's mean against its parent's, so its proposition has the quantifier None, the strength `pole`, and the propositional rendering `SCALARDIM.1.HIGH(CATEGORY.1.3, CATEGORY.1)` (or `NOT ...`). A class-level pole about a restricted subject, or a subject with a relative clause, is invalid, and the planner never draws one. The grammar realizes it as a bare plural with no quantifier, and a plan that gives it a quantifier is an error.
17. **The law-like sets.** A false class-level item is law-like when its quantifier is `nec_all` or `nec_no` and its extensional twin (`all` or `no`) is true: every instance of the subject set has the claimed value, and no rule fixes it. Only the predicate, subject, and quantifier changes of PROPERTY, PART, one-place event-type, and membership facts can give one.
18. **Aspect.** A scene's events carry no aspect. A document draws the aspect of each report from its own part of the `mentions` stream, at `documents.progressive_rate`; with `documents.one_aspect_per_event` (default true), a document reports an event in one aspect wherever it mentions it. A test item draws its aspect from `corpus:grammar:tests:<level>_<change>:aspect`, and its false item keeps the true item's aspect. Both aspects of a report of an event that happened are true. `seen` compares propositions without the aspect, and `stated_propositions` holds event propositions with the aspect `simple`.
19. **Event groundings.** `{scene, step, able, legal, test: "event"}` for a true report, and the same without `step` for a false one. `able` is the able table at the binding; `legal` is whether the event was legal at some time point of the scene, judged on the replayed states with the runtime's `legal`; for an event-type category, both hold when they hold for some leaf event type below it. The test sets split false events into `possible` (`able`) and `impossible` sets, and `meta.possible` is `able`.
20. **The kinds of content.** `documents.content_kind_weights: proportional | equal` (default `proportional`). With `proportional`, the kind of a category document's next sentence is drawn in proportion to the number of facts of each kind that the document can still state, counted once per topic over the plain topic (membership facts, facts about the topic, facts about its subcategories, and relation facts in both roles). `documents.relation_fact_share` still overrides the share of relation facts. The test sets keep an equal chance among the kinds.
21. **Configuration errors.** Every old key is an error that names the new key: `taxonomy` (now `world`), `lexicon.named_proportion.can`, `verb`, and `verb_category`, `quantifiers.all_grounding`, `quantifiers.most.min_proportion`, `quantifiers.generic`, `propositions.events.progressive_rate` (now `documents.progressive_rate`), `scene.verb_weights`, `mention.verb_level_weights` (now `mention.event_level_weights`), and `grammar.morphology.aspect.progressive_rate`.
22. **Scene participants.** With `fluents: {count: 0}` and `scene.policy: uniform_event_type`, a scene has the same participants as the old generator for the same seeds: the tests keep a reference implementation of the old participant draw. The events differ, because the world package draws them among legal events with its own streams and policies, and its steps are Poisson-distributed; the old generator drew a fixed number of events per step.
23. **The statistics.** `sentences.progressive_share` (the share of reports in the progressive) replaces `scenes.progressive_share`; `quantifiers.stated` counts the six quantifiers and `pole`, and `quantifiers.bare_plural` the bare plurals; `quantifiers.strongest_true` is gone, because the stated quantifier is the strongest statable one; `scenes` reports `count`, `steps`, `events`, `mean_changes_per_event`, and `quiescent_share`; `lexicon.event_types_without_word` replaces `lexicon.verbs_without_word`.
24. **The predicate kinds of the logical form stay.** `is`, `has`, `can`, `verb`, `projection`, `scalar`, and `member`, with the keys `feature`, `verb`, `projection`, `pole`, and `category`. Only the labels inside them change. See open question 1.
25. **The truth oracle.** `tests/corpus/truth_oracle.py` reads `definition.json`, `entities.csv`, `derived/capacities.csv`, the embedded taxonomy's `tree.csv`, `roles.csv`, and `categories_generative.csv`, and `config.yaml` of the world run. It judges every proposition with its own code: class-level and instance-level facts over the tables, `able` through `BruteForce` of `semantic_world.world.fixtures` (the derived features and the able columns and matrices), `legal` by replaying a history with the brute-force evaluator, and events over `scenes.jsonl`. It never calls the runtime.
26. **`data/corpus/tiny.yaml` and `default.yaml`** point at `data/world/tiny.yaml` and `data/world/default.yaml` (seed 1). `data/wordforms/corpus_tiny.yaml` and `corpus_default.yaml` are unchanged.

## Changed test expectations

Every expectation that changed, with the reason. Rewritten files are listed by what they now test.

- **`tests/corpus/test_corpus_config.py`** (rewritten): the `world` input, the new keys and defaults, and an error for every old key that names the new key.
- **`tests/corpus/test_corpus_world.py`** (new): defining a world in memory, regenerating and checking a run folder, the world's identity, and the view's agreement with the run's tables (`entities.csv`, `static_features.csv`, `capacities.csv`, `thematic.csv`, the taxonomy's relation matrices, and the defining vectors).
- **`tests/corpus/test_corpus_truth.py`** (rewritten) and **`test_corpus_facts.py`**: the six quantifiers, the felicity rules, the bare plural, the class-level pole without a quantifier, the invalid `nec` cases, and `able` and `legal` in event groundings, against the oracle.
- **`tests/corpus/test_corpus_scenes.py`** (rewritten): scenes are histories; every scene replays on the run's definition; the participants equal the old generator's for the same seeds; the transitive share under `uniform_event_type` is measured over the scenes whose participants have both an able agent and an able pair (the policy draws the other kind when one kind has no available event), with a tolerance of 0.05; the share under `uniform_event` is compared with the able counts of each scene's participants (the old test expected it above 0.6, from a wrong guess that able pairs outnumber able agents); an event type with no able agent (`EVENTTYPE1.2` of the tiny world) is left out of the balance check; the event-type weights are tested on the static world, because in the tiny world with its 4 fluents the preconditions of `EVENTTYPE1.4`, `EVENTTYPE2.1.2`, and `EVENTTYPE2.2.1` are never met from the initial values, so those event types never occur (see open question 4); the counts of a false event's bindings and of events named by a category are taken over 60 and 200 scenes, because two-place events are rare under the preconditions (6% of the default world's events).
- **`tests/corpus/test_corpus_grammar.py`**: every class-level plan of the fixed set carries its quantifier, and `interpret` is given it; the readings of a class-level sentence have its quantifier readings after `generic`, and the words "all" and "no" read as `universal_words` says; the patient capacity of the fixed set is `CANBE.EVENTTYPE2.2.1`, the one of the tiny lexicon that has a word at the named proportion 0.25; four plans that the grammar rejects were added (a class-level plan without a quantifier, a determiner that does not state the plan's quantifier, a pole with a quantifier, and a quantifier on an instance-level plan); the error messages of a report without a tense and of a tense on a capacity are reworded; `propositions.events.progressive_rate` is `documents.progressive_rate`; `named_proportion.can` is `event_unary`.
- **`tests/corpus/test_corpus_realization.py`**: bare plurals come from `plan_for(..., bare=True)` in place of `facts.generic`; the scenes are made known to the truth tests before events are drawn; reports draw an aspect.
- **`tests/corpus/test_corpus_renderings.py`**: `GEN(...)` is `NEC(ALL(...))`, `NEC(NO(...))`, or `ALL(...)`; a class-level pole renders as the category and its comparison class; `NEC` around `MOST` or around an atom is unreadable; the lexicon on the tiny world.
- **`tests/corpus/test_corpus_mentions.py`**: the new labels and the world view; nothing else.
- **`tests/corpus/test_corpus_testsets.py`**: a change gives no false item when every candidate is unstatable in the language, as well as when it is invalid, true, or inexpressible; the role change is counted over 150 scenes and 6 events each (two-place events are rare).

## Open questions for Jon

1. **The predicate kinds of `documents.jsonl`.** The logical form still says `{"kind": "can", "feature": "EVENTTYPE1.3"}` for a one-place event type, `{"kind": "verb", "verb": "EVENTTYPE2.1.2", ...}` for a two-place one, and `{"kind": "projection", "projection": "CANBE.EVENTTYPE2.1.1"}` for a patient capacity. The kinds `is`, `has`, `scalar`, and `member` match their labels better. Should the kinds and their keys be renamed (for example `event_unary`, `event`, `patient_capacity`, and the key `label`) in stage a5b, when the files change anyway, or stay?
2. **The class-level scalar pole.** Choice 16 gives it no quantifier and the rendering `SCALARDIM.1.HIGH(CATEGORY.1.3, CATEGORY.1)`. The alternative is to keep a quantifier-like operator for the reader of the propositional rendering. Jon's call.
3. **The co-occurrence property of stage 6.** On the default configuration (2,500 documents), situational documents' co-occurrence correlates more with thematic relatedness than encyclopedic documents' (0.49 against 0.41 by Pearson, counting words), as before. But encyclopedic co-occurrence now correlates with taxonomic similarity at 0.12, below the situational 0.16, where the old corpus had 0.48 for category documents: with `content_kind_weights: proportional`, about 80% of the sentences of a category document in the default world are relation facts, because a category has far more relation facts to state than facts about itself. With `equal`, the encyclopedic taxonomic correlation is 0.25, above the situational one by Pearson and marginally below it by Spearman. The `check` block of `stats.yaml` reports the property truthfully. Does Jon want the proportional default kept, a cap on the share of relation facts, or the stage-6 property dropped?
4. **Few two-place events under the preconditions.** In the default world, about 6% of the events of a scene are two-place (the old generator drew half), and in the tiny world three of eight event types never occur, because their preconditions are never met from the initial values that the world's rates draw. The narratives therefore say "it swam" far more than "the owl chased the mouse", the event role test sets of the tiny corpus are small (10 and 6 pairs), and the co-occurrence of participants in a situational document is thinner. This belongs with the a5b items on initial rates and the chain example, and perhaps with a check in `define` that every event type occurs in the statistics episodes.
5. **Sibling contrasts and the extensional universals.** With `universal_words: nec`, a fact that holds of every instance without a rule is stated as `most`, and a contrast needs a strong fact about a sibling, so contrasts are rarer (17 in 300 default documents against more than 30 before). `universal_words: either` restores them. Is that the intended trade-off?
6. **`seen` and the aspect.** A true event item is `seen` when a document reports the same event in either aspect, since the aspect is the report's choice (choice 18). The alternative is to count only a report in the item's aspect as seen.
