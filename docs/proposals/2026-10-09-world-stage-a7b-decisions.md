### How the pieces stand today

The corpus says what states hold and how events change them (`HOLDS`, `BECOME`) and what can happen now (`ABLE_NOW`), each at one time point of a scene. The predicate kind `state` names its fluent under `fluent`, every other kind under `label`. A state pair is `changed` when its true item or its false item changed, and nothing records which. Every class-level subject is a `CategoryTerm`; the restrictor of a class-level formula holds categories, literals, and capacity clauses (`ABLE`); no formula binds an event variable. The function words end with `become`. A mention of the JSON logical form holds `instance`, `referent`, `noun`, `restriction`, and `clauses`, and the propositional rendering of a sentence about instances is one conjunction, with no mark on what describes and what asserts. Feature documents are about PROPERTY and PART features, one-place event types, and two-place event types and categories; a sentence of one states a rule at `propositions.rule_statement_rate` or says which categories have the feature. The test sets have 27 sets at five levels.

### Reading of the scope

- A causal statement's **value** is in its predicate (`{"kind": "effect", "label": "BOOLFL.3", "value": true}`), and its polarity is positive, because `nec_all` never takes a negative polarity. On the surface the value is the `not` after `become` or the copula, as it is for a change.
- "**A statement about an event-type category** is true when every event type below the category has the effect or the literal" is read over the leaves below the category, by the definition's entries alone. A category with a word can be the subject of a causal statement whenever the statement holds for every leaf below it.
- "**Encyclopedic documents about a feature gain two kinds of topic**": one-place and two-place event types (and categories of them) are already topics of feature documents, so the new content of a document about one is its causal statements, drawn at `propositions.causal_statement_rate` before the document's other content; a document about a fluent (a base fluent with a word) is new, and all of its content is causal statements, drawn at the same rate (nothing else can be said of a fluent at the class level, so a draw that fails gives no sentence).
- "**The logical form with its descriptions removed**" (the parse-back of the `omitted` rendering) is read as the formula of the sentence's logical form without the parts that its descriptive mentions contribute: a descriptive mention's noun, modifiers, and relative clause propositions. The asserted mentions keep their parts, an indefinite mention inside a descriptive relative clause included, because the sentence introduces that referent. The `omitted` rendering parses back to exactly that formula, so the acceptance criterion holds in both forms.
- "**A false item that held after (or before) every event of its type**" is read non-vacuously: the item is `observed` when at least one event of its type (or of an event type below its category) occurred in the corpus's scenes and the participant in the item's role had the fluent's stated value at the time point after (an effect) or at the time point of (a precondition) every such event.
- **The changes of the causal sets** (predicate, polarity, event, role) are chosen by `test_sets.changes`, as every other set's changes are, so the list gains the two new names `polarity` and `event`, and the default lists all six.

## Engineering choices made while building

Numbered for `docs/DECISIONS.md` (WM.E146 and following). Each is Claude Code's choice inside the stage's scope.

1. **The state predicate's key is `label`** (Jon's ruling 4 on stage a7a). `_JSON_KEY` maps every kind to `label`, and `FLUENT_KEY` is gone: `{"kind": "state", "label": "BOOLFL.3"}` in the logical form, in the oracle, in the tests, and in the guide. The predicates of causal statements use the same key. The `causal` record of a causal statement keeps the specification's keys (`event_type`, `role`, `fluent`, `value`), because it is a grounding record and not a predicate.
2. **Which item of a state pair changed** (Jon's ruling 5 on stage a7a) is recorded as `changed_item` in the metadata of both items of the pair, beside each item's `changed` mark: `true_item`, `false_item`, `both`, or null for a pair of the `_unchanged` set. `stats.yaml` reports, for each state set, `pairs_by_changed_item` with the four counts (`true_item`, `false_item`, `both`, `neither`).
3. **The event term.** The subject of a causal statement is `EventTerm(event_type, role)`, a frozen dataclass beside `CategoryTerm`, with `head` always the generic noun: `{"head": "THING", "event": "EVENTTYPE2.1.2", "role": "patient"}` in the logical form. A proposition's `subject` is a category term, an event term, or an instance; `Proposition.causal` says which, and `causal_record()` gives the `causal` entry that `to_json` writes after `rule`. The value of an effect or a precondition is in its predicate (`Predicate.value`, `{"kind": "effect", "label": "BOOLFL.3", "value": true}`), and the proposition's polarity is positive, because `nec_all` never takes a negative polarity. The kinds `effect` and `precondition` join `KINDS`; the event variable is always `EVENTVAR.1`, because a statement binds one event.
4. **Truth from the definition.** `Truth._evaluate_causal` judges a causal statement by the definition's event-type records: valid when the subject's event type exists and has the role, the fluent is a base fluent (a derived one is refused, with the reason), the value is a boolean, the quantifier is `nec_all`, the polarity positive, and some binding is able (the statement is about some event); true when every event type below the subject's (itself, or the leaves below a category) has the effect, or the precondition literal, with that role, fluent, and value (`Truth.has_entry`). The grounding records the number of event types, how many hold the entry, the able bindings, and `test: definition`. A causal statement is felicitous when the language can state `nec_all`; the bare plural expresses `nec_all` for a causal statement as it does for a rule statement.
5. **The enumeration.** `Facts.causal_statements(event_type, fluent, kind)` lists every true causal statement that the language can state, built once from the definition: for each event type with a word (the one-place event types, then the two-place event types and categories in tree order), each of its roles, each base fluent with a word (`Facts.base_fluents`), each value, and each kind, the statement that `has_entry` makes true and `grounded` confirms. A category of event types with a word states the entries that every leaf below it shares.
6. **The plan of a causal statement.** The subject noun phrase is the generic noun, with "all" or bare, and one relative clause whose single predication is a verb bound to the event variable: `Predication.event` holds `EVENTVAR.1` (`Predication.bound`), its tense is the present, its aspect None; the other participant is the bare generic noun (`bare_things()`), as the object of a subject relative for the agent and as the clause's own subject of an object relative for the patient; a one-place event type takes a subject relative with no object. The main predication is `effect` or `precondition`, its polarity the fluent's value. `event_term_of`, `event_phrase_of`, and `phrase_of(EventTerm)` convert between the plan and the proposition; `SentencePlan.causal` and `proposition()` give `Proposition(CLASS, EventTerm, Predicate(kind, fluent, value=polarity), True, NEC_ALL)`. `check_plan` routes a causal plan to `_check_causal`: `nec_all`, "all" or bare, no modifier on the subject, the clause bound to `EVENTVAR.1` in the present tense with the bare generic noun as the other participant, and a main predication with no object, no event, no time point, and no tense. A bound verb outside the subject of a causal statement, and an effect or a precondition anywhere else, are grammar errors.
7. **The surface.** The realizer says the bound clause with a bare verb (the event branch of `verb_phrase`: the present tense and the missing aspect add no marker, and `can` is never drawn), and records no event for its verb phrase (the record stays None: the words say it). An effect is `become` with the state adjective and a precondition the copula with the state adjective and the function word `before`, a node labeled `Adv`, which stands at the end of the verb phrase under `grammar.word_order.before: after_predicate` (the default) and at its start under `before_predicate`. With number on, the generic noun is plural and the clause's verb agrees with it, as any event's verb does. `interpret` reads a state adjective with a category as its subject as a causal statement (`become` for an effect; the copula and `before` for a precondition; anything else is an error), and then binds the verb of the subject's relative clause to the event variable (`_Reader.bound_subject`); nothing of the record is needed.
8. **The reading `causal`** takes the place of `generic` for a class-level sentence whose verb phrase has `become` or `before`; the quantifier readings of its words follow it, and the `not` of a causal statement never counts as a negated bare plural, because it gives the fluent's value (`_Reader.causal`). `READINGS` and `LEVEL_READINGS` gain `causal`, and `reading_counts` counts it.
9. **The function word `before`** (`BEFORE_WORD`) is appended to `FUNCTION_WORDS` after `become`, so that every function word before it keeps its lexeme label and `can_now`, `are`, `have`, and the inflection words shift by one. The word-form request lists it with the other function words.
10. **The feature documents.** The topics of a feature document gain the base fluents with a word (`Facts.base_fluents`), after the two-place event types, drawn uniformly with the others. A document about an event type or a category draws, for each sentence, a causal statement at `propositions.causal_statement_rate` among those of its topic that it has not stated, before the rule and category content it drew before; a document about a fluent draws a causal statement at the same rate among those about the fluent, and nothing else, so a draw that fails gives no sentence. The section `causal` is the last of the template, after `rule`, so that with `shuffle: 0` the causal statements come last. The draw of the topic among more topics, and the extra draw of the rate, change the feature documents of an existing corpus (see "The tiny corpus against the reference").
11. **The formula and the parser.** A causal statement is `Quantified(nec_all, (BoundEvent(EVENTVAR.1, atom),), Temporal(AFTER|BEFORE, EVENTVAR.1, atom | Not(atom)))`, written `NEC(ALL(EVENT(EVENTVAR.1, EVENTTYPE2.1.2(VAR.1, VAR.2)), AFTER(EVENTVAR.1, BOOLFL.3(VAR.2))))`, with `VAR.1` the agent and `VAR.2` the patient whichever the statement is about, as the specification's examples have it. The parser reads `EVENT(` with an event variable as a bound event (the two-argument form) and `AFTER(` and `BEFORE(` as temporal operators; `proposition_of` reads the role from the variable the scope is about.
12. **Descriptions in the logical form.** Every mention gains `descriptive`: true for a definite mention (`the`), false for an indefinite one (`a`) and for a pronoun, which has no description (`logical.is_descriptive`). The mention's noun, modifiers, and relative clause propositions take the mention's mark; the mentions inside a relative clause take their own.
13. **Descriptions in the propositional rendering.** `formula(form, referents, descriptions)` splits the parts of a sentence about instances into the descriptions (what the descriptive mentions contribute) and the assertion (what the asserted mentions contribute, then the main proposition); a part that an asserted mention contributes too is asserted, and each part is written once. With `marked` (the default) the descriptions come first inside braces, a space, then the assertion (`Described` is the first part of the formula, and `write` joins it with a space); with `omitted`, or when there is nothing to describe, the formula is the assertion alone. `assertion(form)` is the formula with the descriptions removed, which the `omitted` rendering parses back to; the `marked` rendering parses back to the full formula. The tokenizer reads braces, `parse_propositional` reads an opening brace as the descriptions, and `proposition_of` reads the last part as before. A form without `descriptive` marks (one made by hand) renders as before, with no braces.
14. **`seen` and descriptions.** `stated_propositions(documents, descriptions)` counts, with `test_sets.seen.descriptions: false`, only the main clauses, the relative clauses of asserted mentions, and the nouns and modifiers of asserted mentions (`phrase_clause_propositions` gives each clause proposition with its head). The default counts everything, as before.
15. **The causal test sets** are two levels of the builder, `causal_effect` and `causal_precondition`, after `able_now` in file order, each with the changes `predicate`, `polarity`, `event`, and `role`, and each set with a `_lawlike` twin. `test_sets.changes` chooses them, so `TEST_CHANGES` gains `polarity` and `event` (the default lists all six, in the order predicate, subject, quantifier, polarity, event, role, which puts the causal sets in the specification's order), and `LEVEL_CHANGES` maps the causal levels to their four. A true item is a causal statement of the set's kind, drawn uniformly among the world's (a role swap among the two-place ones), with a bare plural at the generic rate as a feature document draws it. The false candidates are another base fluent with a word, the opposite value, another event type with a word of the same arity and tree level (a category for a category), or the other role of a two-place event type; every false item is checked false by the truth tests, which judge it under `nec_all`. An item's `meta` holds `level` as the set's level (`causal_effect`), `causal` (the record, popped from the logical form like `rule`), and `observed`; the record is metadata because a false item has no entry that makes it true.
16. **The `observed` mark.** `Truth.observed(proposition)` replays every known scene: a false causal statement is observed when at least one event of its type (or of an event type below its category) occurred in the corpus's scenes and the participant in its role had the fluent's stated value at the time point after the event's step (an effect) or at the event's time point (a precondition) every time. An observed false item goes into the `_lawlike` twin of its set. A polarity swap is never observed, because the true entry guarantees the opposite value, so the polarity law-like sets are empty by the world's own laws.
17. **The statistics.** `stats.yaml` gains a `causal` block after `states` with `causal_sentences`, `effect_sentences`, and `precondition_sentences`; `by_section` counts the section `causal`; the readings counts list `causal`; a causal sentence counts as a class-level `nec_all` sentence in the quantifier mix.
18. **The configuration keys** `propositions.causal_statement_rate` (0.5), `grammar.word_order.before` (`after_predicate`), `renderings.propositional.descriptions` (`marked`), and `test_sets.seen.descriptions` (true) are read with the specification's defaults, and `data/corpus/default.yaml` lists them; the planner passes the descriptions mode to the propositional rendering of every document sentence and test item, and nothing else reads it.
19. **The independent checks.** The oracle re-reads the `event_types` block of `definition.json` (`Oracle.causal`: every leaf below the statement's event type has the effect or the literal), replays the scenes for the `observed` mark (`Oracle.observed`), and says whether a requirement holds for some binding (`able_somewhere`); `tests/corpus/test_corpus_causal.py` holds the acceptance tests of the stage.

## Open questions for Jon

1. **The topics of feature documents** (choice 10): a fluent is a topic drawn uniformly with the features and event types, and a document about a fluent holds causal statements alone. Should fluent documents have a share of their own, or should the content of a fluent document be read differently?
2. **The `observed` mark is non-vacuous** (choice 16): a false causal item about an event type that never occurred in the corpus's scenes goes into the ordinary set, because nothing was observed about it. The alternative reads "held after every event of its type" as vacuously true and puts such an item into the law-like set.
3. **The causal sets are small** (choice 15): one pair per true causal statement of the world, since a true item is used once per set, so the default corpus has 32 to 87 pairs in the ordinary causal sets and 4 to 24 in the law-like ones, and the two polarity law-like sets are empty. Should a true statement be reused with different false items, so that the sets reach `test_sets.size`?
4. **`test_sets.changes`** (choice 15) gains `polarity` and `event`, and its default lists all six changes. The alternative keeps the four changes of `CORPUS_GENERATOR.md` and makes the causal sets whenever `predicate` and `role` are chosen.
5. **A pronoun's `descriptive` mark** (choice 12) is false: it has no description. The alternative is null.

## Notes for stage a8

Every place where the code now departs from or adds to `docs/specs/WORLD_AND_LANGUAGE.md`, so that stage a8 can update the specification, with Jon's rulings 1 and 4 on stage a7a and the new record of follow-up 2.

- **The tense in the renderings of `BECOME` and `ABLE_NOW`** (ruling 1 on a7a): the renderings write the tense, `BECOME(SCENE.8, TIME.2, PAST, BOOLFL.3(REF.1))` and `ABLE_NOW(SCENE.8, TIME.2, PAST, EVENTTYPE2.1.2(REF.1, REF.2))`, so that every rendering parses back on its own. "States and changes" and "'Can': what is held fixed" write them without the tense: add a note.
- **The key of the state predicate** (ruling 4 on a7a): `{"kind": "state", "label": "BOOLFL.3"}`, not `"fluent"`. "States and changes" writes `fluent`. The predicates of causal statements use `label` too: `{"kind": "effect", "label": "BOOLFL.3", "value": true}`, where "Causal statements" writes `fluent`. The `causal` record keeps `fluent` (`"causal": {"event_type": ..., "role": ..., "fluent": ..., "value": ...}`).
- **Which item of a state pair changed** (ruling 5 on a7a): `changed_item` in the metadata of both items (`true_item`, `false_item`, `both`, null), and `pairs_by_changed_item` in each state set's block of `stats.yaml`. "Test sets" and "Outputs" say nothing of the record.
- **The subject of a causal statement** in the JSON form is `{"head": "THING", "event": ..., "role": ...}` as "Causal statements" writes it; in the code it is an `EventTerm`, a third kind of subject beside a category term and an instance. The proposition's polarity is positive and the value is in the predicate; the specification does not say where the value goes when an effect sets a fluent to false (the surface says `become not`).
- **The `causal` record** is written in a document's logical form after `rule`, and is metadata of a test item (popped from the item's logical form, kept in `meta.causal`), because a false item has no entry that makes it true. "JSON form" says the logical form has it, without saying what a false item holds.
- **Feature documents**: event types were already topics of feature documents, so "gain two kinds of topic" is built as: a document about an event type or a category draws a causal statement at `propositions.causal_statement_rate` before its other content; a fluent (a base fluent with a word) is a new topic, drawn uniformly with the others, whose document holds causal statements alone, drawn at the same rate. The section `causal` is the last of the template.
- **The readings**: `causal` takes the place of `generic` as the level reading of a class-level sentence with `become` or `before` ("Readings" says the sentence "has the reading causal"); the quantifier readings of its words follow, and the `not` of a causal statement never counts as a negated bare plural.
- **The function word `before`** is the seventeenth base function word, after `become`; `grammar.word_order.before` places it at the end of the verb phrase (`after_predicate`) or at its start (`before_predicate`). The node label is `Adv`.
- **The causal test sets** are the builder's levels `causal_effect` and `causal_precondition` (an item's `meta.level` says so, while its logical form is class-level); the changes are chosen by `test_sets.changes`, which gains `polarity` and `event` (the default lists all six); every set has a `_lawlike` twin, whose criterion is the `observed` mark (`meta.observed`), non-vacuous (at least one event of the type occurred); the polarity law-like sets are always empty; a true statement is used once per set, so the sets hold at most one pair per true statement of the world. "Test sets" names the four changes and the `observed` mark, and "Configuration changes" does not list the two new change names.
- **Descriptions**: `descriptive` is false for a pronoun; a part that an asserted mention contributes too is asserted; the braces are followed by one space and then the assertion; a rendering with nothing to describe has no braces; `renderings.propositional.descriptions: omitted` parses back to the formula of the logical form with the descriptive parts removed (`renderings.assertion`), which is how "The propositional rendering still parses back to the logical form" is read for the omitted form. `test_sets.seen.descriptions: false` counts the main clauses and what the asserted mentions contribute.
- **The grounding of a causal statement** is `{"event_types": n, "with_entry": k, "able_bindings": m, "test": "definition"}`; a statement about an event type with no able binding is invalid (the statement is about no event). "Truth" says nothing of a grounding or of vacuity.
- **`stats.yaml`** gains the `causal` block (`causal_sentences`, `effect_sentences`, `precondition_sentences`) after `states`, and `by_section` the section `causal`.
- **The guide's figures** of the default corpus changed as "Changed expectations" says; the datasets in `runs/` are stage a8's to regenerate.
