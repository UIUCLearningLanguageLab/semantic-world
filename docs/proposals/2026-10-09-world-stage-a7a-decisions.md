### Reading of the scope

- "Two-place requirements hold for something like 15 to 20% of pairs on average" is read as the mean, over the two-place event types, of the share of ordered pairs of distinct entities for which the requirement holds (`relation_stats`'s proportion, or the able table's mean). "One-place requirements somewhat stricter" is read as a mean one-place capacity rate below today's 0.69, reached through `event_types.unary.rules`, which are sampling settings and not rules.
- "A third of all events" is `episodes.two_place_share` of `world_stats.yaml`: the share of two-place events among the events of the 1,000 statistics episodes under `uniform_event`. It is measured and reported; nothing forces it.
- "The time point before the participant's first reported event" is `TIME.k` for an event of step k. An initial-state sentence about a participant first mentioned in a sentence that reports events at steps k and later states the participant's fluent at `TIME.k`.
- A result sentence's change "made by that event's own effect" is read strictly: for a base fluent, the change is in the event's own `changes`; for a derived fluent of a participant, the derived fluent differs between `TIME.k` and `TIME.k+1`, and applying this event's own changes alone to the state at `TIME.k` already flips it. A change of a derived fluent that needs another event of the step is not a result sentence of this event.
- The tense of a change and of a blocked sentence is written in the propositional rendering (`BECOME(SCENE.8, TIME.2, PAST, BOOLFL.3(REF.1))`, `ABLE_NOW(SCENE.8, TIME.2, PAST, EVENTTYPE2.1.2(REF.1, REF.2))`), as `HOLDS` writes it, so that every time-pointed proposition parses back from its rendering alone: the specification says that states and changes take the scene's tense and that the JSON form holds it, and the rendering must parse back to the logical form. See open question 1.
- `lexicon.can_words: distinct` gives `ABLE_NOW` the function word with the gloss `can_now` (concept `CAN_NOW`).

### Questions

1. **The tense in the renderings of `BECOME` and `ABLE_NOW`** (above). The specification writes `BECOME(SCENE.8, TIME.2, BOOLFL.3(REF.1))` and `ABLE_NOW(SCENE.8, TIME.2, EVENTTYPE2.1.2(REF.1, REF.2))` without a tense, and `HOLDS(SCENE.8, TIME.2, PAST, BOOLFL.3(REF.1))` with one. The stage writes the tense in all three. Reverting is a change of one writer and one reader in `renderings.py`, and then the tense of a change comes from the configuration rather than from the rendering.
2. **The tense of `become` under an affixal tense.** The word-form pipeline inflects content lexemes only, and this stage does not change it. `become` is a function word, so with `grammar.morphology.tense.realization: affix` it cannot carry the past affix without a form the pipeline never makes. The stage marks `become` for tense when the realization is a separate word, and leaves it unmarked, like the copula, when the tense is an affix. The default grammar marks no tense.

No other question of the orientation changes the design or a file format beyond what the stage prompt lists. The stage proceeds.

**The choice among the candidates.** Three settings were measured with `define` on seeds 1 to 5. (a) One true event-type feature per event type on average (`features: {count: 12, expected_true: 1}`) with the key-lock family at half weight: densities 0.13, 0.19, 0.14, 0.11, 0.24 (mean 0.16), shares 0.27, 0.47, 0.49, 0.28, 0.62 (mean 0.43). Under (c) a two-place event type's requirement is the conjunction of its features' constraints alone, so two event types that share their true features have the same requirement, and one whose true features are a subset of another's has the weaker requirement; the distinct-leaves rule of the tree keeps every pair of event types apart on some feature.

What each lever does (the baseline is the retuned default: share 0.41 over seeds 1 to 3, one-place rate 0.50, density 0.150):

- **The number of event types of each kind** moves the share as a count, with the draws of the world around it: forty one-place event types in place of twenty lower the share to 0.32 (ten give 0.45); seven two-place event types (the code's tree) lower it to 0.14, forty raise it to 0.36 against the baseline's twenty at 0.41, because every event type's own feature draw changes the densities too (0.128 and 0.124 against 0.150).
- **The number of constraints per two-place event type** is the strongest lever: one true event-type feature per event type raises the density to 0.38 and the share to 0.63; six true features lower them to 0.03 and 0.05; six features with three true give 0.11 and 0.28; the own constraint of the code's default gives 0.08 and 0.27. Each constraint removes roughly half the pairs, so the density falls geometrically with their number.
- **The constraint-family weights**: the key-lock family holds for the fewest pairs, so equal weights (the code's default) give a density of 0.08 and a share of 0.30; no key-lock gives 0.18 and 0.43; comparisons alone give 0.18 and 0.43 too.
- **The complexity of the constraints**: the taxonomy's equal operator mix gives a density of 0.11 and a share of 0.35 against the retuned OR-weighted mix's 0.15 and 0.41; arities of 1 and 2 alone give 0.13 and 0.32. A constraint with more OR holds for more pairs, and a simpler constraint neither helps nor hurts much, because the family and the number of constraints matter more.
- **The complexity of the one-place requirements** moves the one-place capacity rate (0.62 with the taxonomy's equal mix, 0.50 with AND weighed 3, 0.34 with AND alone, 0.33 with arities of 2 to 4) and the share by a few hundredths to a tenth (0.37, 0.41, 0.49, 0.51): fewer able agents leave more room for the two-place events.
- **The precondition settings**: with no preconditions at all the share is 0.50, with no feature literals 0.46, with more own literals 0.36, and with a feature rate of 0.6 only 0.15 (0.34, 0.07, and 0.03 on the three seeds). Two-place event types carry the literals of their features beside their own, so preconditions bear on them more than on one-place event types, and feature literals most of all: a literal on a feature blocks every event type of the branch.
- **The scene size** moves the share as the number of ordered pairs against the number of participants: small scenes of 2 to 4 participants give 0.27, large ones of 7 to 11 give 0.59. **`events_per_step`** barely moves it (0.43 at 0.5 and 0.38 at 4): the policy draws one event at a time among what is legal, and more draws per step mostly add one-place events once the few able pairs are taken.
- **`event_type_weights`** moves the share directly under `uniform_event`: two-place event types weighed 3 give 0.64, one-place event types weighed 3 give 0.21.

## Engineering choices made while building

Numbered for `docs/DECISIONS.md` (WM.E129 and following). Each is Claude Code's choice inside the stage's scope.

1. **The retuned settings** are the four of the table above, written into `data/world/default.yaml` with a comment, beside the tree of stage a6; the code's defaults do not change, so every other configuration and the tests' worlds are unchanged. The share reached is `episodes.two_place_share` of `world_stats.yaml`, 0.318 on seed 1. The settings were chosen among three candidates that meet the density target so that every category of event types keeps a word on the default world (see "The choice among the candidates" above).
2. **The levers script** defines the default world with one setting changed and reports three numbers per seed: the two-place share of the 1,000 statistics episodes (`define`'s own, on the `world:stats` stream), the mean one-place capacity rate, and the mean two-place pair density over the able tables. A lever of the scene settings or the event-type weights, which belong to the corpus configuration, runs the statistics episodes again through the episode generator with those settings, as `simulate --config` would. The script takes `--seeds`, `--only`, and `--out`.
3. **Three timed levels.** The corpus's levels gain `state`, `change`, and `able_now` (`propositions.TIMED_LEVELS`), each about one participant of a scene at one time point: `Proposition.time` holds the time point (`TIME.2`) beside `scene` and `tense`, and `aspect` and `event` stay None. The predicate kind `state` (`STATE_KIND`) names a fluent under the key `fluent`, as "States and changes" writes it (`{"kind": "state", "fluent": "BOOLFL.3"}`); every other kind keeps the key `label` (a5b choice 18). The JSON form is the instance-level form with `level`, `scene`, `time`, and `tense`. See open question 4.
4. **The renderings write the tense.** `HOLDS(SCENE.8, TIME.2, PAST, BOOLFL.3(REF.1))`, `BECOME(SCENE.8, TIME.2, PAST, NOT BOOLFL.3(REF.1))`, and `ABLE_NOW(SCENE.8, TIME.2, PAST, EVENTTYPE2.1.2(REF.1, REF.2))` share one form (`renderings.Timed`): the operator, the scene, the time point, the tense, and the atom. A state or a change negates its atom inside (the fluent's value); `ABLE_NOW` is negated outside (`NOT ABLE_NOW(...)`). The parser reads the three operators back, and `proposition_of` gives the proposition with its level. See open question 1.
5. **The verb phrase's record holds the time point.** A sentence's `events` field, which gives the event each verb phrase reports, gives for a verb phrase about a time point the time point qualified by its scene, `SCENE.8.TIME.2` (`histories.time_key`), so that `interpret(tree)` recovers the plan from the tree and the record alone. `Sentence.events` holds `(key, tense, None)` for such a verb phrase, and `is_scene_label` no longer accepts a qualified time point (`is_time_key` does). A test item's `events` entries may be scene labels or time points.
6. **The sentence plan.** `Predication` gains `time` (the qualified time point) and `become` (a change rather than a state); `SentencePlan.level` is `able_now` for a timed predication of an event-type kind, `change` for a timed fluent with `become`, and `state` otherwise; `proposition()` splits the time key. `check_plan` requires a time point exactly for a fluent predicate or an `able_now` predication, an instance subject, a tense, no event and no aspect, and never a time point in a relative clause, so timed sentences take no relative clauses and never appear in one.
7. **The grammar.** A state is the copula with the state adjective (an `A` node), a change `become` (an `AUX` node, a function word) with the state adjective, and an `able_now` sentence "can" (`lexicon.can_words: shared`) or `can_now` (`distinct`) with the verb and its object; `not` follows the auxiliary as it does elsewhere, and gives the fluent's value for a state or a change. The auxiliary of a timed sentence is always said (`grammar.can_rate` applies to capacities only). The tense of a timed sentence is marked on its auxiliary when the morphology realizes the tense as a separate word (`Builder.function` takes marks), and not at all when the tense is an affix, because the word-form pipeline inflects content lexemes only and the stage does not change it. See open question 2.
8. **Reading a tree back.** `interpret` reads a verb with "can" or `can_now` whose record gives a time point as an `able_now` predication, a verb with a record of an event as a report, and a verb with no record as a capacity; a state adjective (a concept with the prefix `BOOLFL.`) needs a time point in its record and gives a state, or a change when its auxiliary is `become`; a static adjective with a time point is an error. A joined relative clause may elide `can_now` as it elides "can".
9. **Readings.** `state` for a verb phrase whose predicate word is a state adjective or whose auxiliary is `become`; for a verb phrase with "can" before a verb, `capacity` and `able_now` under `shared` and `capacity` alone under `distinct`; `able_now` alone for `can_now`; a tense marker beside the auxiliary leaves `able_now` alone, because a capacity has no tense. The level readings are `generic`, `capacity`, `event`, `state`, and `able_now`, in that order, and `reading_counts` counts them all. Under the default grammar, every sentence about an instance with "can" (a capacity or a blocked sentence) is therefore ambiguous between `capacity` and `able_now`, as "'Can': what is held fixed" says, and the first reading of an `able_now` sentence is `capacity`.
10. **The lexicon.** Every fluent, base or derived, is a concept of type `state`, part of speech adjective, in the table's order (after the PART features), with its fluent label as its concept label; `become` joins the base function words at the end of the list, so that the first fifteen keep their order; `can_now` (concept `CAN_NOW`) follows it under `distinct`, before `are` and `have`. The lexeme labels of every concept after the parts shift by the number of fluents, in every corpus.
11. **Truth by replay.** `Truth` judges a timed proposition on the replayed states of its scene (`states_of`, as `legal_in` did): `fluent_value` reads a base fluent from the state and a derived one from `derive` over the scene's participants, cached per scene and time point; `legal_table` caches the legal bindings of every event type among the participants per scene and time point, and `legal_at` answers for an event type or a category (some event type below it); `made_by` says whether one event's own recorded changes, applied alone to the state at the time point, change a fluent (for a base fluent, the change is in the event's `changes`); `caused_by` is the first event of the step that made it. A state's grounding holds `value`, `initial` (at `TIME.1`), and `changed`; a change's `from`, `to`, and `caused_by`; an `able_now` proposition's `able` and `legal`; all with `test: history`. Validity: the scene is known, the time point lies in the scene (a change needs a next time point), the subject and the patient are participants, the tense is the scene's, and the predicate fits the level.
12. **The follow-ups of an event sentence**, in this order, each at its rate: an initial-state sentence for each participant the event sentence first mentioned (in its relative clauses too), at the time point before the earliest event the sentence reports about that participant; a result sentence; a blocked sentence; a description. An initial state draws its polarity at `negation_rate.state` and its fluent uniformly among the fluents with a word that have that value then, falling back to the other polarity. A result draws uniformly among the changes the event's own effects made to its participants: the base fluents in its `changes` that have a word, and the derived fluents with a word whose value differs between the two time points and which the event's own changes alone bring there (`made_by`). A blocked sentence draws its polarity at `negation_rate.able_now` and then uniformly among the bindings whose agent is a participant of the event and whose patient, for a two-place event type, is another participant of the scene, over the event types and categories with a word: for a negative sentence, the able bindings that are not legal at that time point; for a positive one, the legal bindings whose event did not happen in that step. The subject and the patient are the document's next mentions, with static modifiers only.
13. **The test sets.** The item levels are `class`, `instance`, `event`, `state`, and `able_now`, in that file order (`testsets.ITEM_LEVELS`); the state and `able_now` levels take the predicate and subject changes. The kinds are `possible`, `blocked`, and `impossible` for events (a false event that was legal at some time point, one that is able and was never legal, and one that is not able), `changed` and `unchanged` for states, and `blocked` and `impossible` for `able_now`. A true state item is a fluent with a word that holds of a referent of a situational narrative at the scene's final time point, positive; a true `able_now` item is an event type with a word, one-place or two-place with another participant as its patient, that is legal for a referent at the final time point, positive. A state pair goes into the `_changed` set when its true item or its false item is marked `changed`, since either case needs the history to answer (see open question 5); each item carries `changed` in its metadata, and an `able_now` item carries `possible` (whether the binding is able), as an event item does. `input_problems` requires the rendering of a timed item to name its scene and time point.
14. **The statistics.** `stats.yaml` gains a `states` block after `sentences` (the initial-state, result, and blocked sentence counts, the event sentences, the share of event sentences followed by a result, and the share of blocked sentences that are negative); `sentences.by_level` counts the six levels and `negative_share` the class, instance, state, and `able_now` levels; each state set reports `items_changed` and `items_changed_share`; the `readings` counts list `state` and `capacity+able_now`.
15. **The configuration keys** `documents.initial_state_rate` (0.2), `documents.result_rate` (0.5), `documents.blocked_rate` (0.1), `propositions.negation_rate.state` (0.1), `propositions.negation_rate.able_now` (0.8), and `lexicon.can_words` (`shared` | `distinct`) are read with the defaults of the specification; `negation_rate` keeps one default per level. No key of this stage renames an old one, so the renamed-key errors are those of a5a and a6.
16. **The independent checks.** The oracle of the corpus tests (`tests/corpus/truth_oracle.py`) judges states, changes, and `able_now` propositions on the states it replays with the brute-force evaluator, marks `changed` against `TIME.1`, and says whether one event's own recorded changes alone change a fluent (`made_by`); `tests/corpus/test_corpus_states.py` holds the acceptance tests of the stage over the tiny, default, deep, and still worlds.
17. **The reported events of a document** are read from the sentence records' `events` entries that are event labels; the time points that the entries now hold are left out of the pool the event items draw from (the tiny run found the first draw of a time point as an event).

## Open questions for Jon

1. **The tense in the renderings of `BECOME` and `ABLE_NOW`** (choice 4): the stage writes it, as `HOLDS` and `EVENT` do, so that every rendering parses back to its logical form on its own. The specification's examples leave it out. Keep, or drop it and let the tense of a change come from the configuration?
2. **The tense of `become` under an affixal tense** (choice 7): unmarked, because the word-form pipeline inflects content lexemes only. Should a later stage let the pipeline inflect function words, so that `become` (and the copula) can take the past affix as the specification's "marked like any verb's" reads?
3. **The own constraint** (the retune): the chosen settings turn `event_types.binary.own_constraint` off, so a two-place event type's requirement is the constraints of its features alone, and siblings that share their true features share their requirement. The alternative that keeps the own constraint and meets the density target (one true event-type feature per event type) leaves most categories of event types without a word and their events all but absent from the scenes. Is the own constraint off acceptable as the default, or should a category with an empty base relation get a word anyway, which would reopen the first alternative?
4. **The key of the state predicate** (choice 3): `fluent`, as "States and changes" writes it, while every other predicate names its symbol under `label` since a5b. Keep the specification's key, or make it `label` for uniformity (a one-constant change)?
5. **Which state pairs are `changed`** (choice 13): a pair whose true item or false item changed. The alternative is to mark by the true item alone.
6. **The share reached** (the retune): 0.27 on the default world (seed 1), 0.43 on average over seeds 1 to 5, with the spread following the densities. Is the default world's share the target, or the mean?
