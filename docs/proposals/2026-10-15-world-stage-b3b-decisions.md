### How the pieces stand today

Main holds stage b3a (061f8e3, merged as PR #28). The corpus states scalar poles as comparisons and comparatives over the static scalars, with the machinery of b3a: the predicate kinds `comparison` and `comparative`, `Truth.compare` over the runtime's `greater`, `above`, and `below`, the `Comparison` formula, the `COMPARATIVE` mark, and `comparative_plan`. ### Reading of the scope

- **"Every proposition records its cut"** is read as a `cut` on the predicate of a numeric pole (0.75 or 0.25, the language's cut for that pole), written in the JSON predicate and in the rendering (`ABOVE(NUMFL.1, REF.1, RANGE, 0.75)`), unlike the relative poles' `z`, which is one value for the language and sits on the form (ruling 4). The cut differs by pole, so it belongs to the predicate.
- **"Which proportion applies"** is read as a concept type of its own, `numeric`, with `lexicon.named_proportion.numeric` (default 1.0), whose unit is the two poles of one numeric fluent, as `scalar` names both poles of a dimension together.
- **"The surface is become with the comparative"** for `RAISES` and `LOWERS`, and "became hungrier" for `INCREASE`, is read with the high adjective's comparative for `RAISES` and `INCREASE` and the low adjective's for `LOWERS` and `DECREASE`: the two adjectives of a numeric fluent are its two directions, and the sentence's word is recorded under `word` as for a comparative.
- **"The condition joins the restrictor"** is read with the realizable subset the grammar allows: a static literal on the subject's role as a modifier of the subject (a positive PROPERTY as an adjective, a PART as a with-phrase or a without-phrase, a negated PROPERTY in the subject's relative clause, which only an agent statement has as a subject relative), a static literal on the other role as a modifier of the bare "things" inside the relative clause, a Boolean fluent literal on the subject's role as a verb phrase with `before` joined to an agent statement's subject relative, and a Boolean fluent literal on the other role as a relative clause with `before` on the inner noun phrase. A conditional effect whose condition the grammar cannot realize (a negated PROPERTY or a fluent on the patient of an object relative, or a numeric literal, decision 23) gets no statement, and `stats.yaml` counts the effects left unstated and why.
- **"INCREASE and DECREASE are judged on the step's net change"**: a result sentence states a numeric change when the event's own effect moved the value (a recorded change with the event as its cause) and the value at `TIME.k+1` against `TIME.k` has the effect's direction; the proposition's truth is the net change alone, so a test item or another sentence about the same step is true whatever caused the change.
- **"The world package should not change in this stage"**: it does not; the corpus reads `Effect.condition`, `Effect.amount`, and the replayed states.

## Engineering choices made while building

Numbered for `docs/DECISIONS.md` (WM.E224 and following). Each is Claude Code's choice inside the stage's scope.

1. **The comparison partners (Jon's ruling 2 on stage b3a).** `documents.comparison_partners` chooses the categories a category document compares its topic with: `siblings` (the default), the topic's siblings with a word; `thematic`, as many thematic partners (at least one), the other named categories of the topic's level whose leaves are most related to the topic's leaves; or `both`, the pool of stage b3a. `Facts.comparison_partners` reads the setting, and nothing else about the comparison facts changes. Under the default the category documents' thematic co-occurrence falls back below its value before stage b3a (0.11 against 0.17; "The co-occurrence figure" below).
2. **The word of a comparative test item (Jon's ruling 3 on stage b3a).** `TestSetBuilder._pair` draws the word once per pair, the high or the low pole's with equal chances, from the pair's own part of the tests stream, `corpus:tests:<set>.<pair>:word`, so that the two items of a role-swap pair use one word and the draw changes nothing else; the proposition is unchanged, the form records the word under `word`, and with the low word the lesser argument is the subject (the mentions are made first, as before, and swapped). The class-level comparison facts in `class_predicate` and `class_role` draw their word the same way.
3. **The numeric adjectives are a concept type of their own.** `numeric`: the two poles of a numeric fluent, `NUMFL.<n>.HIGH` and `.LOW`, adjectives, named together as the two poles of a scalar are, at `lexicon.named_proportion.numeric` (default 1.0, listed in `data/corpus/default.yaml`). They stand after the scalar poles and before the generic noun in the lexicon, so the lexeme numbers of the generic noun and the function words move by two per numeric fluent (the tiny world's "the" is `LEXEME.46`, not `LEXEME.42`). The concept type `state` stays the Boolean fluents'.
4. **A numeric pole is a comparison with `RANGE`.** Kind `comparison`, label the pole, comparison class `RANGE`, operator `ABOVE` (high) or `BELOW` (low), stated at a time point of a scene and never timelessly or of a category (the truth tests and `check_plan` refuse both). The language's cut for the adjective, `numeric_adjectives.cut_high` (default 0.75) or `cut_low` (default 0.25), as a fraction of the range (`cut_low` must be below `cut_high`), is recorded in the JSON predicate under `cut`, after `class`, by `logical.with_cut`, and written in the rendering, `HOLDS(SCENE.8, TIME.2, PAST, ABOVE(NUMFL.1, REF.1, RANGE, 0.75))`; a negative state puts `NOT` before the comparison. The cut is no part of the `Proposition` in code, as the z is not (ruling 4 on stage b3a): `Truth.cut(pole)` gives it, and `Truth.compare` takes a state and a threshold for a numeric dimension. The `z` of a form is written only for a scalar pole (`has_pole` leaves numeric poles out), so a numeric state has no `z` unless a mention carries a scalar pole.
5. **A numeric comparative is a state.** Kind `comparative`, label the fluent, patient the other instance, level `state`, `HOLDS` around `GREATER`, the sentence's word under `word` as for a scalar comparative, never negated; `Predication` carries the time, and `SentencePlan._comparative` gives the state-level proposition. A narrative's comparative description (`Planner._comparative`) draws uniformly among the scalars with words on which the two participants' values differ and the numeric fluents with words on which their values differ at the event's own time point, `TIME.<step>` (`Facts.instance_comparison_at`), from the same stream and at the same rate, `documents.comparative_rate`; the state at the event's time point is the one the event sentence is about, and the plan carries `SCENE.8.TIME.2` and the scene's tense.
6. **A numeric change has a direction.** Kind `state`, label the numeric fluent, `direction` `increase` or `decrease`, level `change`, never negated; the rendering is `INCREASE(SCENE.8, TIME.2, PAST, NUMFL.1(REF.1))` or `DECREASE`, with the tense written as `BECOME` writes it (Jon's ruling 1 on stage a7a: every rendering parses back on its own). The truth is the net change of the step, the value at `TIME.k+1` against `TIME.k`, whatever moved it, judged by the runtime's `increase` and `decrease` through `greater`; the grounding records `from`, `to`, and `caused_by`, the first event of the step whose own recorded change of the fluent has the direction, or null when the drift alone moved it. `Planner._result` states a numeric change only when the event's own recorded change of a fluent with words has a direction and the net change has the same direction, so a change by drift alone is never a result sentence, and a change the event made but the drift undid is not stated. The surface is `become` with the comparative form of the pole whose direction it is (`NUMFL.1.HIGH` for an increase, `.LOW` for a decrease), which `Predication` holds as its label (`comparative_form`, `direction`), as a comparative's predication holds the adjective it uses; `mentions.timed_predication` makes it from the proposition.
7. **Initial states draw among Boolean fluents and numeric poles alike.** `Planner._initial_state` draws, at the state negation rate, among one pool: the Boolean fluents with a word and the numeric poles with words that have the drawn polarity at the time point (`Truth.predicate_holds`), the other polarity when none has it. A numeric pole's state says the copula and the adjective, as a Boolean state does ("was hungry", "was not full").
8. **`RAISES` and `LOWERS`.** Kind `effect`, label the numeric fluent, `direction` `raises` or `lowers`, no value: true exactly when every event type below the statement's event type has a numeric effect of that sign on that role under the statement's condition (none for an unconditional statement), judged by `effect_matches` in `Truth.has_entry`; a precondition statement about a numeric fluent is invalid (decision 23). The rendering writes `RAISES(EVENTVAR.1, NUMFL.1(VAR.1))` in place of `AFTER`, the surface is `become` with the comparative form of the pole of the direction ("become hungrier", "become fuller"), and the causal record carries `direction` in place of `value`. The numeric fluents with words join the fluent topics of feature documents (`Planner._feature_topics`), whose content is the statements that raise and lower them, one per sentence as for a Boolean fluent. In the causal sets the predicate swap of a numeric statement is another numeric fluent with words and the polarity swap the other direction; a numeric statement is `observed` when every event of its type recorded a change of the fluent in its direction (`Truth.recorded_direction`), which is about the effect, not the net change, so a direction swap is never observed, as a polarity swap is not, and the polarity sets keep no law-like twin. A conditional numeric effect makes a conditional `RAISES` or `LOWERS` statement under choice 9, so that the rule "no statement true by a conditional effect without its condition" holds for every effect.
9. **The conditional causal statements.** `Condition(role, label, value)` literals, a static feature (PROPERTY or PART, free or determined) or a Boolean fluent (base or derived) of a role of the event, kept on `EventTerm.condition` in one order (the role, then PROPERTY, PART, BOOLFL, then the number), written in the JSON subject under `condition` and repeated in the `causal` record. In the rendering the condition joins the restrictor after the bound event, a static literal as an atom of the role's variable (`NOT` before a negated one) and a fluent literal as `BEFORE(EVENTVAR.1, BOOLFL.2(VAR.2))`, and `_causal_proposition` reads them back; the bound event stays first. The statement is true exactly when every event type below its event type has the effect, Boolean or numeric, under exactly that condition (`condition_of` turns the world's literals into the corpus's; an effect under a numeric threshold condition gives none, decision 23); the statement without its condition is false, because the definition guarantees the effect only when the condition held, so it is never enumerated as true and `test_no_causal_statement_is_true_by_a_conditional_effect` still holds. The statements are enumerated from the leaves' effects (`Facts._conditions`): for each event type with a word, role, fluent with words, and value or direction, the distinct conditions of the matching effects of the leaves below, the empty one first, each tested with `has_entry` over every leaf, so a category's statement holds when every leaf has the effect under the same condition (an inherited effect brings its condition to every leaf). `Truth.observed` counts only the events whose participants satisfied the condition at the event's time point (`condition_held`: a static feature by the entity's value, a Boolean fluent by the replayed state), so the false items of a conditional statement can be law-like.
10. **The realization of a condition.** On the noun phrase of its role, in one way (`event_phrase_of`, `_things_under`): a static literal of the subject's role as a modifier of the subject (a positive PROPERTY as an adjective, a PART as a with-phrase or a without-phrase, a negated PROPERTY in the subject relative, "things that are not red and strike things"), a fluent literal of the subject's role as a predication of kind `precondition` joined to the subject relative ("things that strike things and are awake before"), and a literal of the other role on the bare "things" inside the relative clause, as a modifier or as a relative clause of its own with `before` ("things that strike things that are asleep before", "things that things that are asleep before strike become broken"). A statement about the patient is an object relative, which holds one verb and joins no negated literal, so a fluent literal or a negated PROPERTY literal of the patient's role has no place in it (`grammar.realizable_condition`): such a conditional effect makes no statement, the enumeration skips it, a role swap that would need it is no candidate, and `stats.yaml` counts the conditional effects of the event types with events that make no statement, by reason (`numeric_condition`, `patient_relative`, `no_word`), under `causal.conditional_effects`. `event_term_of` and `interpret` read the condition back from the phrases (`bound_subject` binds the verb and keeps the `before` predications), `check_plan` demands the one realization (the subject must equal `event_phrase_of` of its term), and the class-level relative clause of a causal plan may hold `before` predications (`_check_predication` with `causal`). The readings are unchanged: the main clause has `become` or `before`.
11. **The condition change.** `test_sets.changes` gains `condition` (listed in `data/corpus/default.yaml`), which drops the condition of a conditional effect statement; the sets are `causal_effect_condition` and `causal_effect_condition_lawlike`, after the role sets of the effect statements (`LEVEL_CHANGES` of `causal_effect` alone; a precondition statement has no condition). The format check of a pair treats `condition` as a field a form holds only when it has one, like `clauses` and `z`, and the literals of one condition as of one notation (`_CONDITION_LABEL`), because a condition mixes features and fluents by the world's design; the tests' own `shape` check does the same.
12. **The state sets.** `_draw_state` draws among the Boolean fluents with a word and the numeric poles with words at the scene's final time point; a numeric pole's `changed` mark (the grounding's `changed`) is whether the pole holds at the final time point and not at `TIME.1`, or the reverse, so that a value that drifted while staying above the cut counts as unchanged, as the initial state already answers it; its predicate swap is another numeric pole. The new set `state_comparison_role` (the builder's level `state_comparison`, after the state sets, the role change only, ordinary kind only) draws a situational narrative, two of its referents, and a numeric fluent uniformly, a comparative at the final time point oriented true, and its false item swaps the two participants; its items' verb phrases record the final time point as the state items' do.
13. **The tense affix on the auxiliary.** With the tense realized as an affix, the auxiliary of a sentence about a time point carries it, joined to its token (`LEXEME.176-PAST`): the copula and `become` of states and changes, and `can` (or `can_now`) of what was possible then, the same auxiliaries that carry the tense word when the tense is a word; `_Builder.function` joins an affix as `word` does (one at most). `interpret` and the readings already read the affix on the auxiliary (a capacity has no tense, so "can-PAST" leaves `able_now` alone). The word-form request's `inflect` gains entries that name function words by gloss, `{function_words: [is, become], affixes: [PAST]}`, after the entry of the content lexemes of the same affix, the glosses in the order of the request's function words; `takes` is unchanged, because the requirement on a function word follows from the entry itself. The default language has no tense, so the default request is unchanged apart from its new lexemes.
14. **The one change to the word-form pipeline.** An `inflect` entry may name function words (`InflectEntry.function_words`, read from a request file or the configuration by `_read_inflect`, one of `words`, `lexemes`, and `function_words`; each gloss must be one of the request's function words, checked in `_read_closed_class`). A function word so named is drawn among the forms that can take the entry's affixes (`closed_class.can_take`: the join passes the phonotactic check with the repairs and is not a common English word; an English function word that cannot take its affix is an error), its inflected forms are made right after the function words (`function_word_pairs`, `FUNCWORD.<n>.AFFIX.<m>`, by `add_inflected` in `add_function_words` for a run with a request, and in `add_closed_class` otherwise), and in a run without a request the affixes are drawn before the function words, from their own stream as before, so that the function words can be filtered; `ClosedClassConfig.function_inflect` and `function_requirements` give the entries and the requirements. The content words, the assignment, and their audio and embeddings are the same with and without the entries (`test_inflected_function_words`), and `render` needs no change: an inflected function word's label is its function word's label with the affix's, which `WordForms.form` already builds. Nothing else of the pipeline changes.
15. **The statistics and the figures.** `stats.yaml`: `states` gains `numeric_state_sentences` and `numeric_change_sentences`; `causal` gains `raises_sentences`, `lowers_sentences`, `conditional_sentences`, and `conditional_effects` (the world's conditional effects of event types with events, the stated, and the unstated by reason); `comparisons` gains `numeric_comparative_sentences`. The snapshot's corpus figures carry the `causal` and `comparisons` blocks whole and the two numeric counts of `states`.
16. **The oracle.** `tests/corpus/truth_oracle.py` judges every numeric form on its own replay (the brute-force evaluator's numeric values) and the `range` of the fluent's symbol record: a numeric pole at the form's `cut`, a numeric change by the values at the two time points, a numeric comparative at the time point with the form's word, `RAISES` and `LOWERS` by the sign of the record's numeric effect, and a conditional statement by the effect whose condition literals equal the form's (`_effect_matches`); its `observed` counts the events whose participants satisfied the condition, and a numeric statement's by the event's recorded change; `changed` takes the form's cut for a numeric pole, and `made_by` a direction for a numeric fluent.

## Open questions for Jon

1. **A numeric literal at the language's cut** (item 6 of the stage prompt, not built). A conditional effect whose condition is a numeric threshold literal makes no statement (choice 10, decision 23): in the default world 6 of the 10 conditional effects are such, so they are the larger part of what the corpus leaves unsaid. When a literal's threshold is exactly the language's cut (`> cut_high`, or `<= cut_low`), the numeric adjective says exactly what the literal says: "hungry things that eat things become full" for `when agent.NUMFL.1 > 0.75 then ...`. Should such a literal be stated with the adjective as a condition (a `before` predication of kind `comparison`), with every other threshold still unstated? The world draws its thresholds from `event_types.preconditions.numeric_thresholds` (the quarter points by default), so the match is common; the proposition would be exact, because the adjective's comparison is the literal.
2. **Conditional `RAISES` and `LOWERS`** (choice 8). The stage prompt defines `RAISES` and `LOWERS` by an unconditional numeric effect; the stage states a conditional numeric effect as a conditional statement, under the rule of item 6, so that every conditional effect, Boolean or numeric, is stated when its condition can be said, and the unconditional reading is false for every one. Is the extension right, or should a conditional numeric effect make no statement?
3. **A fluent or a negated PROPERTY condition of the patient in a statement about the patient** (choice 10) has no place in the grammar, because an object relative holds one verb and the noun phrase one relative clause ("things that things strike" cannot take "that are asleep before"). The default world has no such effect, and the tiny world one (`when NOT patient.PROPERTY.4 then patient.BOOLFL.2 := 1` on `EVENTTYPE2.2.2`). Should the grammar gain a second relative clause on a noun phrase, or a way to say such a condition, in a later stage?
4. **The numeric pole's `changed` mark** (choice 12) is whether the pole's truth differs between `TIME.1` and the final time point, not whether the value moved, because the drift moves nearly every value. Is the mark right?
5. **The tense affix on `can`** (choice 13). The stage prompt names `become` and the copula; the stage puts the affix on every auxiliary of a sentence about a time point, `can` and `can_now` included, as the tense word already stood beside every one ("could not eat"). Should `can` stay bare?

## Notes for phase (c)

What the writer of phase (c)'s specification (stored fluent relations, their profiles, and locations) must know about where the corpus stands after phase (b).

- **Every predicate kind of the corpus and where it is judged.** `property`, `part`, `event_type1`, `member`, `patient_capacity`, `event_type2` (the static kinds, judged on the world's tables and the fixed test); `comparison` (a scalar pole against a class, timeless, or a numeric pole against `RANGE` at a time point); `comparative` (`GREATER` of scalars, timeless, or of a numeric fluent at a time point); `state` (a Boolean fluent at a time point, or with `become` a change; a numeric fluent with a `direction`, a change); `effect` and `precondition` (the causal statements, with `direction` for a numeric effect and the `condition` on the event term). A stored relation of phase (c) is a two-place fluent, which none of these kinds covers: a state of a pair at a time point (`HOLDS(SCENE.8, TIME.2, PAST, RELATION.1(REF.1, REF.2))`) would be a new kind beside `state`, with its own change, its own causal statements (an effect on a relation), and its own test sets, and the truth tests would read it from the replayed state as `fluent_value` reads a Boolean fluent.
- **The timed levels** (`state`, `change`, `able_now`) are about one participant and one time point of a scene; a comparative at a time point is the first timed proposition with two participants, and shows the way: the patient is a participant of the scene, the plan carries the time on the predication, and the rendering puts the two-place comparison inside `HOLDS`.
- **The restrictor of a causal statement** holds the bound event and, since this stage, the condition's literals; a precondition on a relation, or an effect that sets one, would add literals with two variables.
- **The grammar's limits** that phase (b) ran into: one relative clause per noun phrase, an object relative with one verb, a comparative never in a relative clause, modifiers static. A relation ("near") as a modifier of a mention would need a time point, as a fluent modifier would (decision 20: modifiers stay static).
- **The word-form pipeline** needs no change for new content words; new function words (a word for a relation, or a locative adposition) join `FUNCTION_WORDS` at the end, so the words before them keep their order, and the pipeline draws them after the assignment.
