### How the pieces stand today

The taxonomy writes the old labels everywhere, draws the CAN rules after the IS and HAS rules from `taxonomy:rules`, and builds the verb tree, the constraints, the projections (with exposure), and the relation statistics from `taxonomy:verb_tree`, `taxonomy:constraints`, and `taxonomy:pairs`. The world embeds a taxonomy run and relabels its objects in `world/labels.py`; the corpus reads the world through `corpus/world.py`, which translates at its boundary and wraps the taxonomy's fixed test and rule terms. The truth oracle of the corpus tests keeps a label table of its own. The word-form pipeline's `branch_of` reads both label forms.

Checked before the first change: the three golden folders of the regression test are byte-identical to the a5a generator's outputs, so the golden folders are the a5a reference. Two golden folders were added for `relations.yaml` and `tiny_relations.yaml` (their noun files only), so that the scalar configurations have a reference too.

### Plan for the stage

1. **The taxonomy relabels** itself: `CATEGORY.<path>`, `INSTANCE.<path>.<k>`, `ISA.CATEGORY.<path>`, `PROPERTY.<n>`, `PART.<n>`, `SCALARDIM.<n>`, in every file and expression. The configuration keys `features.is` and `features.has`, and the internal type names `is` and `has` (the `type` column of `features.csv` and `feature_stats.csv`), do not change: only labels are in the scope of "Labels".
2. **The taxonomy loses CAN features, verbs, and exposure.** `features.can`, `rules.overrides.can`, and `verbs` fail with errors that name `event_types.unary`, `event_types.unary.rules`, and `event_types.binary` in the world configuration; a rule file that applies a template to `can` or gives an explicit `CAN` rule fails with an error that names the event file. `instances.csv` becomes `base.csv` (label, leaf, free PROPERTY and PART features, scalars), and the derived static features stay in `derived/static_features.csv`. The streams `verb_tree`, `constraints`, and `pairs` leave the taxonomy.
3. **The machinery moves into the world package**: `world/event_tree.py` (the event-type tree, from `verbs.py`), `world/constraints.py`, `world/projections.py` (without exposure), `world/relation_stats.py` (the moved computation, with `event_type` for `verb`), and `world/unary.py` (one-place requirements, sampled as the taxonomy sampled CAN rules). `world/statics.py` builds the static side of a world: the taxonomy run, the one-place requirements over an extended feature table, the event tree, the relations, the capacities, and the relation statistics. Explicit requirements from an event file replace sampled ones before the capacities and statistics are computed.
4. **Streams.** The world gains `world:requirements`, `world:event_tree`, `world:constraints`, and `world:pairs`, seeded from the world's seed. The world's draws change as a result, and every change is listed.
5. **The translation goes**: `world/labels.py` is deleted, `corpus/world.py` reads the world's objects directly, the truth oracle loses its label table, `episodes.py` and the fixture generator read the new labels only, and `wordforms.assign.branch_of` takes the new form only.
6. **Defaults**: `features.base_rate_heterogeneity: 2`; `event_types.binary.density` defaults to the degenerate check only (an event type is resampled when its requirement holds for no leaf pair or for every leaf pair); `constraint_min_density: null`; `documents.content_kind_weights: equal`.
7. **Starting rates** in `definition.json`, on each base fluent's symbol (`initial_rate`), read back by the runtime definition for `scene.initial: redraw`; `world_stats.yaml` loses `fluents.initial_rates`.
8. **The chain example** gets explicit requirements, checked on seeds 1 to 10.
9. **Never-legal event types**: `define` redraws the own precondition literals of an event type that was never legal in the statistics episodes, from the part `world:preconditions:<label>`, up to a bounded number of rounds, and reports the redraws and whatever is still never legal in `world_stats.yaml`. The default precondition settings are retuned toward a two-place share of a third to a half.
10. **The predicate kinds** of `documents.jsonl` become `property`, `part`, `scalar`, `member`, `event_type1`, `event_type2`, and `patient_capacity`, each with the key `label` (the scalar predicate keeps `class`, the two-place predicate keeps `patient`).
11. **Fixtures** regenerated (`make-fixtures` and `tests/world/hand_world.py`), the README updated for the symbol's `initial_rate`.
12. **Tests**: the regression test is retired and replaced by a relabel test over the golden folders and new golden hashes; the taxonomy's verb tests move to `tests/world/` or are retired, each listed below; every other test moves to the new labels.
13. **Documents**: this file, `docs/DECISIONS.md`, the three guides, `CLAUDE.md`, and the fixture README.

### Reading of the scope

- "The same world as before, label for label" is read as: for a taxonomy configuration with the same settings (heterogeneity off, as the golden configurations record it), the categories, the free and determined PROPERTY and PART features, the instances, the scalar values, and the IS and HAS rules are those of the a5a run with the labels mapped. The CAN rules and columns leave the taxonomy, so they are left out of the comparison, and `similarity.csv` and `feature_stats.csv` change where they summed over the CAN columns.
- The leaf similarity that weighs participants (WM.E59) is computed over the taxonomy's generative vectors, which no longer hold CAN columns. The world's episodes and the corpus's scenes therefore draw other participants than in a5a. The alternative, adding the one-place capacities of each category back into the vectors, would keep a taxonomy-level statistic dependent on world-level draws; it is not taken.
- The one-place requirements are drawn from a world stream of their own rather than by continuing the taxonomy's `rules` stream, so that the taxonomy's rule count never changes a requirement. The requirements of the default and tiny worlds therefore differ from a5a's.

## Engineering choices made while building

Numbered for `docs/DECISIONS.md` (WM.E97 and following). Each is Claude Code's choice inside the stage's scope; the two that touch what a reader of the files or the specification sees beyond the stage prompt are flagged as open questions at the end (choices 10 and 16).

1. **The relabel is the taxonomy's own.** The label prefixes live in `taxonomy/config.py` (`LABEL_PREFIX = {is: PROPERTY, has: PART}`, `SCALAR_PREFIX`, `CATEGORY_PREFIX`, `INSTANCE_PREFIX`), and `features.py`, `instances.py`, and `tree.py` make the labels from them. The configuration keys `features.is` and `features.has`, the type names `is` and `has` in the `type` column of `features.csv` and `feature_stats.csv`, and `rules.overrides.is` and `.has` do not change: "Labels" relabels objects, not settings. `Tree.ancestor_label(category, level)` replaces the two places that rebuilt an ancestor's label from indices. The similarity-bound scope `is_has` now equals `all`, since the taxonomy has no other binary features.
2. **The moved keys fail with errors that name their new place.** `features.can` names `event_types.unary`, `rules.overrides.can` names `event_types.unary.rules`, and `verbs` (present with any value) names `event_types.binary`, all in the world configuration; a rule file with `applies_to: can`, or an explicit rule whose output is `CAN.<n>` or `EVENTTYPE1.<n>`, fails with an error that names the world's event file (`event_types.event_file`), where a one-place requirement is given by hand. The example rule file's CAN template and explicit `CAN.3` rule became a PART template and an explicit `PROPERTY.35` rule.
3. **`base.csv` replaces `instances.csv`.** `label`, `leaf`, the free PROPERTY and PART features in matrix order, and the scalars with six decimals. The determined features stay in `derived/static_features.csv`. The category-vector files keep both kinds of feature, as "Taxonomy outputs" says. The verb files, `projections.csv`, `constraints.yaml`, and `relations.yaml` are no longer written by the taxonomy. The thermometer file keeps its name, `instances_scalar_codes.csv`.
4. **The golden folders are the a5a reference.** Before the first change, the three golden folders of the regression test were checked byte for byte against the a5a generator, and golden folders for `relations.yaml` and `tiny_relations.yaml` were added (their noun files only). `tests/taxonomy/test_taxonomy_golden.py` runs the relabeled generator on each golden run's resolved configuration without its CAN and verb keys (and, for the rule-file run, on the old rule file without its CAN entries), and compares, label for label: `tree.csv`, `roles.csv`, `features.csv`, the three category files without their CAN columns, `instances.csv` against `base.csv` plus `derived/static_features.csv`, the IS and HAS entries of `rules.yaml` (inputs, expressions, truth tables, layers, families, thresholds), `feature_stats.csv` without its CAN rows, and the counts of `summary.yaml`. `similarity.csv` is not compared, because its vectors summed over the CAN columns. The same file holds the new golden hashes of every file of every example configuration (`golden_hashes.json`, rewritten by `tests/taxonomy/make_golden_hashes.py`) and the byte-identical-folders test. `test_taxonomy_regression.py` and `golden_relations_hashes.json` are retired.
5. **The verb machinery moved as world modules, plus two new ones.** `taxonomy/verbs.py` became `world/event_tree.py` (`EventTree`, `generate_event_tree`, feature type `event_feature`, labels `EVENTFEAT.<n>`, tree prefix `EVENTTYPE2.`), `taxonomy/constraints.py` became `world/constraints.py` (roles `agent` and `patient`, labels `CONSTRAINT.<owner>`, `Relations` over an `EventTree`, `generate_relations(binary, scalars, rules, tree, instances, rng_constraints, rng_event_tree)`), `taxonomy/projections.py` became `world/projections.py` without exposure, and `taxonomy/relation_stats.py` replaced the world's translating wrapper (`event_type` for `verb`, `RelationStats.frames()`). `world/unary.py` samples the one-place requirements, and `world/statics.py` bundles the static side (`StaticWorld`: the taxonomy run, the requirements, the event tree, the relations, the capacities, the relation statistics) and builds it in order: requirements, constraints and tree, explicit requirements, capacities, statistics. The dynamics (`event_types.py`), the definition, the capacities, the episodes, and the corpus's `World` read the `StaticWorld`. `WorldResult` holds `statics` and no longer a second taxonomy run (`taxonomy_run`), since the taxonomy is never modified now. `EventType` lost `source_label`.
6. **The one-place requirements live in an extended feature table.** `UnaryRequirements.features` is the taxonomy's feature table with one determined feature per one-place event type appended (type `event_type1`, label `EVENTTYPE1.<k>`, the layer above the taxonomy's last), and `UnaryRequirements.rules` the taxonomy's rules followed by the requirements, so that the taxonomy's machinery (evaluation, cones, the fixed-by-rule test behind `capacity_roles.csv` and the corpus's `NEC` statements, the explicit-rule parser) runs over the requirements unchanged. The taxonomy never writes these features; the world writes their columns as the one-place capacities.
7. **The world draws the event types from streams of its own.** `world:requirements` (the one-place requirements), `world:event_tree` (the event-type features and tree), `world:constraints` (the constraints, and the samples of the capacities' local test), and `world:pairs` (the relation statistics' pairs and estimates) join the stream table before `world:fluents`, seeded by the world's seed; the taxonomy's `verb_tree`, `constraints`, and `pairs` streams are gone. A one-place requirement is sampled as the taxonomy sampled a CAN rule (the pool of every PROPERTY and PART feature of the types with positive weight and the scalars, the arity, the function, the duplicate check against the taxonomy's rules and the other requirements, the variance bound), with `event_types.unary.rules` over the taxonomy's `rules` settings. Every requirement is sampled first and the explicit ones replace sampled ones afterwards, so an event file changes no other event type's draw. The world's draws therefore differ from a5a's for the same seed, and the fixtures, the tiny world's statistics, and every corpus figure changed with them. `WorldStreams.part(name, label)` gives the generator of a named part of a stream.
8. **The world configuration is typed.** `event_types.unary` is read into `UnaryConfig(count, rules)` (`count` defaults to 20, the old CAN count; `rules` are sampling overrides over the taxonomy's `rules` block, and the arity is checked against the requirement pool) and `event_types.binary` into `BinaryConfig`, the old `VerbsConfig` without `expose_agent` and `expose_patient`, read against the taxonomy configuration the world names (its `rules` sampling and its scalars). `projections` in `binary` still fails with the error that points to views. `Config.taxonomy_run` holds the validated taxonomy configuration; nothing is merged into the taxonomy file any more. A run's `config.yaml` shows every resolved value under `event_types.unary` and `event_types.binary`.
9. **The density defaults.** `event_types.binary.density` defaults to `{min: 0.0, max: 1.0, max_tries: 200}`, and a leaf-pair density of exactly 0 or 1 is always outside the range (`DensityConfig.inside`), so the default redraws only an event type whose requirement holds for no leaf pair or for every leaf pair, as "A principle for defaults" says; the old range stays a setting, and `density: null` turns the check off. `constraint_min_density` defaults to null. A constraint whose leaf-pair density is exactly 0 or 1 is resampled too, up to `max_tries`, whenever the event-type check is on: a feature constraint that no leaf pair satisfies makes every event type that carries its feature impossible, which the event-type check then cannot repair by redrawing the event type's own constraint (in the tiny world the tuner failed on several seeds for that reason, with event types of density 0). This is the one place where the stage goes beyond the letter of "never resamples a constraint for its density": it resamples a constraint for being degenerate, not for its density. `event_type_stats.csv` carries `leaf_pair_density` and `tries` by default, since the check is on by default.
10. **An explicit requirement keeps only the ancestors' base relations.** A two-place event type with an explicit requirement keeps the constraints of the event-type features that are defining at an ancestor (its parent's base relation, REL.10) and loses the constraints of its other true features together with its sampled own constraint (`Relations.explicit`). Under the a5a semantics, which kept every true feature's constraint, the chain example could not complete on every seed: "catch" and "eat" carried different non-defining features, so their pair sets were disjoint on seeds 2, 5, and 6 even with equal explicit requirements. With this semantics two siblings with equal explicit requirements have equal pair sets. The event type's `features` list in `definition.json` still names every event-type feature true for it: `features` says what is true of the event type, and `requirement` what it entails. See open question 2.
11. **The chain example's requirements.** Both event types of `data/world/events/chain_example.yaml` require the agent to be bigger than the patient on the first scalar (`agent.SCALARDIM.1 - patient.SCALARDIM.1 > 0.0`), so whatever is caught can be eaten. On seeds 1 to 10 of `data/world/tiny_chain.yaml` the able pairs of the two event types coincide, and both event types occur in the statistics episodes of every seed.
12. **Heterogeneous base rates by default, and equal ones in the tiny taxonomies.** `features.base_rate_heterogeneity` defaults to 2. `data/taxonomy/tiny.yaml` and `tiny_relations.yaml` set it to null, with a comment: with 6 free features of a type, a heterogeneous draw left the first superordinate of seed 7 with no true feature, and the similarity bound (phi, undefined against a constant vector) could then place no second superordinate. `default.yaml` and `relations.yaml` take the default.
13. **The leaf similarity is over the taxonomy's vectors.** Participants are weighed by the taxonomic similarity of leaves computed from `categories_generative.csv`, which no longer holds CAN columns. The one-place capacities of the categories are not added back.
14. **Starting rates in `definition.json`.** Every fluent symbol carries `initial_rate`: a float for a base fluent, null for a derived one; other symbols have no such key. The rate is part of the hashed `symbols` table, so every identity changed. `RuntimeDefinition.initial_rates` reads and validates it, `EpisodeGenerator` takes its rates from the definition when none are given, `simulate` reads nothing from `world_stats.yaml`, and `world_stats.yaml` no longer reports `fluents.initial_rates`. The hand world's base fluents carry 0.5, which no hand case uses.
15. **The never-legal redraw.** `define` always runs the statistics episodes (the `episode_stats` keyword of a5a is gone, because the episodes decide the redraws), then, for every event type that was never legal in them, is not never-able (no entity or no ordered pair meets its requirement), and has no explicit precondition, draws its own precondition literals again from its own part `world:preconditions:<label>` (`event_types.redraw_preconditions`): the inherited literals stay, the number of own literals comes from `preconditions.literals`, and a literal that duplicates an inherited one or would make one of the event type's own effects empty is drawn again. The world is rebuilt and the episodes rerun, up to `MAX_REDRAW_ROUNDS = 5` rounds; the agreement test runs once at the end. `world_stats.yaml` reports `episodes.precondition_redraws` (label to count), `episodes.never_legal` as a mapping from label to the reason (`never_able` or `preconditions`), a warning per event type still never legal, and `episodes.two_place_share`. In the tiny world the three event types of the a5a notes are redrawn (once, twice, and once) and every event type is then legal; in the default world nothing is redrawn and every event type is legal. The corpus's in-memory world therefore runs the 1,000 episodes too (about 5 seconds for the default world).
16. **The precondition defaults are unchanged.** The two-place share of the default world's statistics episodes is 5.7% under the defaults and cannot reach a third by precondition settings: with no preconditions at all it is 11.9%, because the default world has 20 one-place event types with a mean capacity rate of 0.69 against 7 two-place event types whose requirements hold for 0.1% to 16% of ordered pairs, so a scene of 5 participants has about 69 able one-place events against 8 able two-place events. The shares reached: `feature_rate: 0.0` 11.2%, `feature_rate: 0.1` 6.9%, `literals: {0: 0.5, 1: 0.4, 2: 0.1}` 12.3%, both 8.5%, and agent-only roles 5.7%. The settings stay as the specification lists them, and the levers that would reach a third are listed as open question 3.
17. **Fixtures regenerated.** `tests/world/hand_world.py` writes the fluent symbols' `initial_rate`, and the twelve hand fixtures and the six tiny fixtures were regenerated and pass the runtime and the brute-force evaluator; the fixture README describes the new field.
18. **The predicate kinds of the logical form** (Jon's ruling 1) are `property`, `part`, `event_type1`, `event_type2`, `patient_capacity`, `scalar`, and `member`, each naming its symbol under `label` (`propositions.LABEL_KEY`); the scalar predicate keeps `class`, the two-place predicate `patient`. The Python constants keep their names (`IS`, `HAS`, `CAN`, `VERB`, `PROJECTION`) with the new values, so that the corpus's hundreds of uses did not change; their docstrings say so. The corpus's `World.features` and `RuleTerm.kind` use the same kinds (`property`, `part`, `event_type1`).
19. **`documents.content_kind_weights` defaults to `equal`** (Jon's ruling 3), with `proportional` as the option. On the default corpus the stage-6 co-occurrence property holds under the default by both correlations and both counts: situational documents' co-occurrence with thematic relatedness is 0.52 (words, Pearson), 0.55 (Spearman), 0.54 and 0.58 by referents, against encyclopedic documents' 0.15, 0.18, 0.16, and 0.19; their co-occurrence with taxonomic similarity is 0.14 (words, Pearson), 0.14 (Spearman), 0.17 and 0.16 by referents, against encyclopedic documents' 0.48, 0.34, 0.48, and 0.34. Nothing was tuned.
20. **No translation anywhere.** `world/labels.py` is deleted; the corpus's `World` reads the static side and the definition; the truth oracle of the corpus tests reads the run's files as they are; `episodes.py` reads the category vectors with the prefixes `ISA`, `PROPERTY`, `PART`; `read_scene_settings` rejects `verb_weights` with an error that names `scene.event_type_weights`; `wordforms.assign.branch_of` reads the new form only (an ID without a prefix and numbers has no branch). The comments of `data/wordforms/default.yaml` and the examples of the word-form guide use the new labels.
21. **Test support.** The corpus tests' deep and still taxonomies became world configurations over taxonomy configurations without CAN and verb keys, and every test that compared the world with the taxonomy's verbs compares it with the static side.

22. **A duplicate draw is a failed try of the density tuner.** The tuner redraws an event type's non-defining features when its density is outside the range; a draw that duplicates another event type's vector (with distinct leaves required) used to stop the tuning as "stuck", so under the degenerate-only default 17 of the 74 two-place event types of seeds 0 to 9 of the default world kept a requirement that holds for no leaf pair, after 1 to 67 of the 200 tries. A duplicate now counts as a failed try, and the tuning stops only when nothing can change (every feature defining at the parent). The survey then has 5 degenerate event types in 74. The default world of seed 1 is unchanged; the tiny world's rule set changed (its event types' feature vectors), and the tiny fixtures were regenerated again; the tiny corpus's figures did not change.
23. **Cosmetic.** The moved modules' locals and helpers say `event_type` where they said `verb`, and `build_tree`'s docstring names the world's event-type tree.
24. **A bare plural that states `no` or `nec_no` is negated.** The corpus tests over the default world found a `nec_no` membership fact said as a positive bare plural (`CATEGORY.3.2.2 IS A CATEGORY.4.1.2`): the realizer negated by the predication's polarity alone, and a `nec_no` fact has a positive polarity. The realizer now negates the main clause of a bare plural whose quantifier is `no` or `nec_no` ("penguins are not fish"), the interpreter reads such a negation back as the quantifier (polarity true), and a negated bare plural's readings are the counterparts of what a bare plural expresses (`most` for `most`), with `nec_no` added for a membership predicate, since a bare plural states `nec_no` for membership facts only (a5a choice 12). The fixed set's negated bare plural therefore reads `most` alone, where it read `nec_all` and `most` before.

## Retired, moved, and rewritten tests

Every test that was retired, moved, or rewritten, by file, with its reason. "Relabeled" means the test's subject is unchanged and only its labels moved to the new ones. The whole suite (`pytest`) passes: see the stage report for the counts.

## Open questions for Jon

1. **The taxonomy's type names and keys.** `features.is` and `features.has`, `rules.overrides.is` and `.has`, and the `type` column values `is` and `has` keep the old names while the labels say `PROPERTY` and `PART` (choice 1). Should the keys and type names follow the labels (`features.property`, `features.part`, `type: property`)? It is a small, mechanical change, but it touches the configuration format and the corpus's lexicon keys `named_proportion.is` and `.has` (WM.E80).
2. **What an explicit requirement keeps** (choice 10). The stage keeps the constraints of the features defining at the event type's ancestors and drops the others. The a2 ruling kept every true feature's constraint; that reading cannot guarantee the chain. Is the new reading the intended one, or should the chain example instead be allowed to give its parent category (`EVENTTYPE2.1`) a base relation by hand?
3. **The two-place share** (choice 16). No precondition setting brings the default world's two-place share near a third; the ceiling under `uniform_event` is about 12%, set by the requirements (20 one-place event types against 7 two-place ones, and few able pairs among a scene's participants). The levers that would reach a third are fewer one-place event types (`event_types.unary.count`), denser two-place requirements (`event_types.binary.density.min`, or fewer or simpler constraints), larger scenes, or the balanced policy `uniform_event_type`, which gives half by construction. Which, if any, should the default take?
4. **Degenerate constraints** (choice 9). The stage resamples a constraint whose leaf-pair density is exactly 0 or 1 by default, beyond the letter of "never resamples a constraint for its density". Keep?
5. **The redraw bound** (choice 15). Five rounds of 1,000 episodes each; each round redraws every event type still never legal. Enough?
6. **A relations block in `world_stats.yaml`.** The taxonomy's `summary.yaml` reported the verb block (constraint-family counts, the share of approximate projections, whether proportions were estimated, pair shortfalls, event types outside the density range); a world run reports none of it, apart from the density warnings, since `RelationStats.pairs_short` and `estimated` live in memory only. Should `world_stats.yaml` gain a `relations` block with those entries?
