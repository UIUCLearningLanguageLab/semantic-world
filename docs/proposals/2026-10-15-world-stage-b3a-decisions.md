### How the pieces stand today

Main holds stage b2 (166e6a9, merged as PR #27). The world's runtime computes the comparisons (`greater`, `above`, `below`, `increase`, `decrease`) over entities and category means, with `ENTITY` for all entities, and `comparison_classes(definition)` holds every class's count, mean, and population standard deviation; `derived/comparison_classes.csv` holds the same figures. The corpus still computes its own classes (`Truth.pole_mask` and `_class_scalar`: the mean and standard deviation over `World.below(category)`, with `THING` for every entity) and states a scalar pole in the form of the old corpus: an instance-level predicate of kind `scalar` with its comparison class (`SCALARDIM.1.HIGH(REF.1, CATEGORY.1.5)`), a class-level predicate of kind `scalar` with no quantifier whose comparison class is the parent or `THING` and is written in the logical form for the reader only (`SCALARDIM.1.HIGH(CATEGORY.1.3, CATEGORY.1)`), and a pole literal in a restriction relative to the term's own category. No proposition records its z (`scalar_adjectives.z` is the language's setting, read by `Truth`). The oracle (`tests/corpus/truth_oracle.py`) recomputes the poles itself with `THING`. There are no comparatives, no function word `than`, and no comparative morphology. The default world draws no derived Boolean rule over a numeric threshold on seeds 1 to 5 at `rule_weight: 1`.

### Reading of the scope

- **"The corpus reads every comparison class from the world"** is read as: from the runtime's `comparison_classes(definition)`, not from `derived/comparison_classes.csv`. The corpus defines its world in memory from a configuration file, and a corpus over `world: {config: ...}` has no run folder to read a derived file from; the runtime's table is the same figures, cached on the definition, and the agreement of the two is stage b2's test. The corpus's own computation is removed.
- **"A class-level pole is ... the predicate kind `comparison`"** is read for both levels: an instance-level pole is the same comparison (`ABOVE` or `BELOW` of the referent against the noun's category), so it takes the same kind. The kind `scalar` of the old corpus is replaced by `comparison` everywhere (open question 1 below records the alternative).
- **"Every proposition records its z"** is read as a key `z` of the JSON logical form of every sentence and test item that holds a pole, in its predicate, in a restriction, or in a mention's modifiers, and of every pole atom of the propositional rendering. The z is the language's (`scalar_adjectives.z`), so it is no part of the identity of a `Proposition` in code, as the comparison class of a class-level pole is now, and `from_json` and the parser read it back for the check that it is the language's.
- **"`GREATER` as the only comparative operator, with 'smaller than' as the language's word for `GREATER` with its arguments in the other order"** is read as: the proposition is `GREATER(SCALARDIM.1, x, y)` with `x` the greater; a sentence plan names the adjective it uses, `SCALARDIM.1.HIGH` ("bigger") with `x` as its subject, or `SCALARDIM.1.LOW` ("smaller") with `y` as its subject, and the plan's proposition is the one form. Which word a sentence uses is a choice of discourse: in a narrative the subject is the event's agent, in a category document the topic, and a test item takes the "bigger" word with the greater argument as its subject, so that a role swap exchanges the two noun phrases and a predicate swap changes the adjective's dimension and nothing else.
- **"A comparison fact: the topic compared with a sibling or a thematic partner, on one scalar"** is read as a partner drawn uniformly from a pool fixed per topic: the topic's siblings, and as many thematic partners among the other categories of the topic's level, those whose leaves are most related to the topic's leaves by the world's thematic relatedness. The scalar is drawn among those with a word on which the two means differ.
- **"At `documents.comparative_rate` (default 0.1), on a scalar where their values differ"** is read as a sentence after an event sentence of a two-place event, about the event's two participants, drawn like the other follow-up sentences, from a part of the propositions stream of its own.

## Engineering choices made while building

Numbered for `docs/DECISIONS.md` (WM.E207 and following). Each is Claude Code's choice inside the stage's scope.

1. **The rule weight (follow-up of b2, Jon's ruling 4).** `numeric_fluents.rule_weight` defaults to 21, in the configuration reader and in `data/world/default.yaml`: the smallest whole weight at which the default world (seed 1) draws a derived Boolean fluent whose rule reads a numeric threshold (20 draws none; 21 gives `BOOLFL.8` a numeric literal). At 21, seeds 1 to 5 of the default world get 1, 0, 1, 1, and 1 such fluents (`BOOLFL.8` on seeds 1, 3, and 4, `BOOLFL.7` on seed 5). The weight changes the draw of every derived rule from `world:fluents`, so the Boolean rules, the rule-set identities, and the baselines of every off setting move for both worlds (listed under "Changed expectations"). The corpus states such a fluent like any other derived Boolean fluent, judged on the replayed states.
2. **The comparison classes come from the runtime.** `Truth.comparison_class(category, scalar)` reads the runtime's `comparison_classes(definition)` (the figures of `derived/comparison_classes.csv`, cached on the definition), not the file: the corpus defines its world in memory from a configuration file, and a corpus over `world: {config: ...}` has no run folder to read a derived file from. The generic noun maps to `ENTITY` (`comparison_class_of`), and `World.below` accepts `ENTITY` beside `THING`. The corpus's own computation (the mean and standard deviation over `World.below`) is removed; `Truth.pole_mask(pole, class)` keeps the vectorized mask of every instance against a class, cached per pole and class, from the runtime's statistics, and a test checks that it equals the runtime's `above` or `below` for every entity. `test_the_corpus_computes_the_same_classes` now checks that the corpus's classes are the world's on every row.
3. **One predicate kind for poles.** The old kind `scalar` becomes `comparison` at both levels: an instance-level pole and a class-level pole are the same comparison (`ABOVE` or `BELOW` of the referent, or of the category's mean, against a class), so they take one kind; the concept labels `SCALARDIM.<n>.HIGH` and `.LOW` stay the labels of the adjectives. The predicate carries its comparison class at both levels (`class`), which is part of the proposition now that `class_levels_up` lets it vary, so `from_json` no longer strips it, and the JSON predicate writes its `operator` for the reader (derivable from the label).
4. **The z is recorded on the logical form.** A JSON logical form that holds a pole anywhere (its predicate, a restriction of a category term, or a modifier of a mention) records the language's threshold under `z`, after `predicate`; a form without a pole has no `z`. The z is the language's (`scalar_adjectives.z`), so it is no part of a `Proposition` in code: `from_json` and the parser read it back only to check it. The propositional rendering writes it in every pole atom (`ABOVE(SCALARDIM.1, REF.1, CATEGORY.1.5, 1.0)`, Python's shortest round-trip form). The format check of a pair treats `z` as it treats `clauses`, a field held only when there is something to hold, because a mention's modifier can be a pole in one item of a pair and not in the other.
5. **The comparative predicate.** Kind `comparative`, label the dimension (`SCALARDIM.1`), patient the other argument (an instance, or a plain category term), operator `GREATER`, polarity always positive: a negated comparative is invalid, because the language says the opposite with the arguments the other way round. A comparative needs words for both poles of its dimension (the two are named together). A class-level comparative compares two plain categories' means, never `THING`, never a restricted term.
6. **One proposition, two words.** A sentence plan says a comparative with `Predication(COMPARATIVE, <pole adjective>, object)`: the high pole's adjective ("bigger than") puts the subject first in `GREATER`, the low pole's ("smaller than") the object first (`Predication.low`, `mentions.comparative_plan`). The JSON logical form holds the sentence's subject and object in the sentence's order and records the adjective under `predicate.word`, as it records whether a mention is descriptive; `Proposition.from_json` and the propositional rendering read the word and put the arguments in the proposition's order, so a form said with the low pole's word names the greater argument as its patient. Which word a sentence uses is a choice of discourse: a narrative's comparative description takes the event's agent as its subject, a category document its topic, and a test item the high pole's word with the greater argument as its subject, so that a role swap exchanges the two noun phrases and a predicate swap changes the dimension alone.
7. **The class-level pole's comparison class is in the plan.** `Predication.standard` holds it, and `interpret(tree, ..., standard=...)` takes it from the record, as it takes the quantifier of a class-level sentence: the words do not say it. An instance-level pole takes its class from the noun, and `standard` must be None.
8. **The comparative grammar.** "The owl is bigger than the mouse" is the copula, the pole adjective with the mark `COMPARATIVE` (an affix joined to its token, `LEXEME.39-COMPARATIVE`, or a function word before or after it, by `grammar.morphology.comparative: {realization: affix | word, position: after | before}`, default `affix` and `after`, with no `enabled` flag because the form is always available), and the standard: a `PP` of the function word `than` and the object noun phrase (`NP-OBJ`), placed where a verb's object stands (inside the verb phrase when verb and object are neighbors, a daughter of `S` otherwise), with `than` before the noun phrase or after it by `word_order.adposition`. `than` is the eighteenth base function word, appended after `before`, so every earlier function word keeps its label; `COMPARATIVE` joins the inflection words of the lexicon when it is realized as a word. A comparative is never negated and never in a relative clause, and `check_plan` says so.
9. **The rendering forms.** A `Comparison` formula (`ABOVE(dimension, x, class, z)`, `BELOW(...)`, `GREATER(dimension, x, y)`) replaces the pole atom; a category mean is written `MEAN(CATEGORY.1.4)`; the parser reads the operators and `MEAN`, and refuses the old atom `SCALARDIM.1.HIGH(REF.1, CATEGORY.1.3)`. A pole in a restrictor is `ABOVE(SCALARDIM.1, VAR.1, <the term's category>, z)`, with `ENTITY` for the generic noun. `proposition_of` reads a `Comparison` back at either level.
10. **The truth tests.** `Truth.compare` judges every comparison with the runtime's `greater`, `above`, and `below`, over entity indices or `Mean(category)`, at the language's z; `_class_comparison` requires the class to be a strict ancestor of the subject's category or `ENTITY`, and a plain subject; `_class_comparative` requires two distinct plain categories; an instance-level comparative requires two distinct instances. The groundings carry `z` for poles and `other` (the second value or mean) for comparatives.
11. **Facts.** `Facts.comparison_class(category)` is the ancestor `class_levels_up` levels above the category, or `ENTITY` when the tree runs out (CG.3); `Facts.scalars` are the dimensions with words; `comparison_fact(a, b, scalar)` and `instance_comparison(x, y, scalar)` give the true `GREATER` one way or the other, and None on a tie; `comparison_partners(topic)` is the pool a category document compares its topic with: the topic's siblings with a word, then as many thematic partners (at least one) among the other named categories of the topic's level that are not its siblings, in order of the mean thematic relatedness of their leaves to the topic's leaves, ties in tree order.
12. **The planner.** Category documents gain the content kind `comparison` (`COMPARISON_KIND`), drawn with the same chance as the other kinds (WM.34) when the topic has a partner and a scalar has words: a partner and a scalar in a random order, the first pair whose means differ and whose fact the document has not stated; the fact's section is `characteristic`, its strength `comparative` (beside `pole`), it takes no sibling contrast, and `proportional` weights count its available facts. In narratives, after an event sentence of a two-place event, a comparative description of the two participants follows at `documents.comparative_rate`, after the blocked sentence and before the description, on a scalar drawn uniformly among those with words where the two values differ, with the agent as its subject; its two draws come from the new stream `corpus:comparatives`, a part per document, so that the other draws of a document are unchanged when the rate is 0 (the sentences inserted still move what follows them, as for every corpus feature).
13. **The test sets.** `instance_comparison_predicate` and `instance_comparison_role` are the sets of the builder's level `instance_comparison`, after the instance sets, ordinary kind only: a narrative document, two of its referents, and a scalar are drawn uniformly, the comparison is oriented so that it is true, the predicate swap takes another dimension on which it is false and the role swap exchanges the arguments. A comparative takes no subject swap and no quantifier swap. The class-level comparison facts join `class_predicate` (another dimension) and `class_role` (the arguments swapped) through the test sets' draw of class-level facts, whose role swap now draws a relation fact or a comparison fact. A test item's object noun phrase avoids the same features as its subject, so the two items of a role-swap pair mention an instance alike whichever part it plays.
14. **The word-form request and the pipeline.** With the comparative realized as an affix, `wordform_request.yaml` lists the affix `COMPARATIVE` (a suffix for `position: after`, a prefix for `before`), `takes.adjective: [COMPARATIVE]` (the pole adjectives share their part of speech with the other adjectives, so every adjective's word must take the affix), and the pole lexemes that appear inflected under `inflect`; `than` is among the function words. The pipeline makes the affix from the request without a change under its default source. The one change to the pipeline: `ENGLISH_AFFIXES` maps `COMPARATIVE` to *-er* (`ER0`, one form), so that `closed_class.affixes.source: english` accepts the corpus's request; without it, every default corpus would be refused under that option.
15. **The statistics.** `stats.yaml` gains the block `comparisons`: `comparison_sentences` and `comparative_sentences` by level (`class`, `instance`), `comparative_descriptions` (the narratives' sentences of the section `comparative`), `pole_modifiers` (mentions with a pole among their modifiers), and `pole_restrictions` (class-level subjects with a pole); `quantifiers.stated` gains the key `comparative`. The corpus figures of the snapshot gain the block.
16. **The configuration.** `documents.comparative_rate` (default 0.1), `grammar.morphology.comparative` (`{realization: affix, position: after}`; an `enabled` key is an error), and `scalar_adjectives.class_levels_up` (default 1, at least 1), each listed in `data/corpus/default.yaml` and written by `resolved()`.
17. **The oracle.** `tests/corpus/truth_oracle.py` recomputes every pole and comparative from the `SCALARDIM` columns of `entities.csv` alone: a pole against the mean and the population standard deviation of the rows below the class (every row for `ENTITY`) at the form's `z`, a class-level pole over the mean of the subject set, a comparative over two rows' values or two categories' means, strictly, with the form's `word` putting the arguments in the proposition's order; a form without a `z` is judged at the oracle's own, so that the older tests, which pass bare propositions, still run.

## Notes for stage b3b

What the builder of the numeric fluents in the corpus, `RAISES` and `LOWERS`, the conditional causal statements, and the tense affixes on function words must know about where things stand.

- **The comparison machinery is in place.** `Predicate` of kind `comparison` (a pole, with `comparison` the class) and `comparative` (`GREATER`, with `patient` the other argument); `Truth.compare(operator, dimension, x, y, standard)` calls the runtime's `greater`, `above`, and `below`; the `Comparison` formula of `renderings.py` writes and parses `ABOVE`, `BELOW`, and `GREATER` with `MEAN(...)` arguments. The numeric fluents' comparisons (`ABOVE(NUMFL.1, REF.1, RANGE, 0.75)`, `GREATER(NUMFL.1, REF.1, REF.2)` at a time, `INCREASE`, `DECREASE`) need a time point and a state: the runtime functions take `state`, `Truth.states_of(scene)[time - 1]` gives it, and the timed levels (`HOLDS`, `BECOME`) are the place for them; a numeric adjective is an absolute pole at the language's cut (`numeric_adjectives.cut_high` and `cut_low`), so a new predicate kind or a `standard` of `RANGE` with the cut as its threshold is needed, and the `z` of a form is the relative poles' threshold, not the cut.
- **Words for numeric fluents.** `World.fluents` and `Facts.fluents` are the Boolean fluents; a numeric fluent needs two adjective concepts (`NUMFL.<n>.HIGH` and `.LOW`, concept type to decide) with the comparative forms of this stage (`comparative_label`, `comparative_plan`, and the `COMPARATIVE` mark work for any `SCALARDIM`-like label; `pole_dimension` and `pole_operator` split a label at its last period, and `_kind` in `renderings.py` and `interpret.py` read the `SCALARDIM.` prefix, which a `NUMFL.` prefix must join).
- **The comparative description** of a narrative (`Planner._comparative`) is timeless and about the static scalars; a comparison of two participants on a numeric fluent at a time point joins it, at the same rate, with a `time` on the predication and `HOLDS` around `GREATER`.
- **`RAISES` and `LOWERS`**: `Truth.has_entry` and `_evaluate_causal` read `unconditional_effects`, which leaves numeric effects out; a numeric effect is `effect.numeric` with the sign of `effect.amount`; the oracle's `entries` leaves out an effect with an `amount`. The causal sets' changes (`_causal_candidates`) need a direction swap.
- **Conditional causal statements**: `Effect.condition` holds `ConditionLiteral`s of kind `feature`, `fluent`, or `numeric`; `has_entry` ignores conditional effects; the restrictor of a causal statement is one `BoundEvent`, and `_causal_proposition` in `renderings.py` requires it, so the condition joins the restrictor there.
- **Tense affixes on function words**: `_Builder.function` in `realize.py` attaches inflection words to a function word but never an affix (`timed_marks` of a timed sentence are given to the auxiliary); `takes` in `request.py` lists the parts of speech that take affixes, and function words are not among them.
- **The oracle** judges comparisons at the form's `z` and with the form's `word`; a numeric comparison at a time point needs the replayed numeric values, which `Oracle.numerics(scene)` gives.
- **The reference runs** of this stage are in `/Users/jon/Documents/Projects/semantic-world-reference/b3a/` (the tiny and default worlds and corpora from `main` at 166e6a9, before the stage).
