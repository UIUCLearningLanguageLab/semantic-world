### How the pieces stand today

Main holds stage c4b. The corpus states pair changes (results with `become`), narrates moves and acts, and mentions places as referents told apart by place properties and parts; the predicate kind `pair_state` at the level `state` is built end to end (JSON, rendering, `interpret`, readings, `Truth._evaluate_pair_state`, the oracle's `state`) and no sentence makes one; `Truth.legal_at` and `legal_table` hold the place event types' bindings over the scene's places; `Truth.compare` and the runtime's `comparison_classes` cover place scalars and place categories since stage c1; the grammar refuses a place as the subject of any sentence; the test sets leave out every sentence of c4b by three filters (`_reports` without place events, `entity_referents`, and `candidates` and `changes_for` returning nothing for `pair_state` and `place_event_type`). The c4a proposal's items 4 to 6 and 8 (the causal statements with pair literals) stand for stage c5a.

The places' tables show what ruling 2 needs: at `z = 1.0`, in a category of two places, the place with the greater value is `HIGH` and the other `LOW` whenever the two values differ (each lies exactly one standard deviation from the mean), so the tiny world's two places of each category are told apart by their one place scalar, and most of the default world's categories by one of their two.

### Plan for the stage

In the order of the scope:

1. **Initial states.** `Planner._initial_state` gains, beside the Boolean fluents and the numeric poles, the participant's place on each location fluent with a preposition, its pairs on the pair fluents between entities with a preposition toward the entities already mentioned, and `SAME` with the entities already mentioned (ruling 1); a negative location state names a place already mentioned.
2. **Place poles.** `MentionRules.place_preference` gains the place scalars; a place pole is a modifier relative to the noun's category, read through `Truth.place_pole_mask`; `Truth` judges instance-level propositions about a place.
3. **Place descriptions.** `documents.place_description_rate` (default 0.1); after an event sentence that mentions a place, a description of that place by a place property, a place part, a category of places, or a place pole; the grammar admits a place as the subject of such a sentence and of a comparative of places, and of nothing else.
4. **Blocked place events.** `Planner._blocked` ranges over the place verbs and the scene's places; a move blocked by the compiled `move` literal alone is skipped, by a legality test that leaves out that literal (`legal_without` in the runtime).
5. **Comparatives of places.** After a sentence that introduces a second place of the scene, at `documents.comparative_rate`, a comparison of the new place with a place already mentioned on a place scalar where they differ.
6. **Test sets.** The filters undone; the change `object`; the state sets' role swap for pair fluents between entities that are not symmetric; the event and `able_now` sets with place event types; the draws of a change restricted to the items it applies to.
7. **Outputs.** The new counts of `stats.yaml`, the figures, and a check that `scenes.jsonl` holds the histories with places and pairs (it does: the tiny run's first scene lists `places`, `initial_places`, `initial_pairs`, and the pair changes of its moves).
8. **The runs.** The reference run first (from main before any change, into `semantic-world-reference/c4c/`); at the end the tiny and default worlds (identities unchanged) and corpora regenerated, rendered with the existing word forms if the requests' lexemes are unchanged, the snapshot and the guides' figures rewritten.

### Reading of the scope

- **"The participant's place on each location fluent."** A positive location state names the participant's own place, mentioned or not (a first mention takes `a`: "a mouse was at a dry place", the specification's example). A negative one ("the owl was not at the wet place") names a place of the scene already mentioned, as a pair state between entities names a referent already mentioned: "was not at a place" would introduce a place by denying something of it.
- **"`SAME` with a referent already mentioned."** `SAME.PAIRFL.k(x, y)` for every `SAME` preposition, `x` the participant and `y` an entity of the scene already mentioned, true when the two have the same partner on the functional pair fluent `k`, at the same place for a location fluent. It is a state, never a change: no one event records a change of `SAME`, so `BECOME` over it is refused (choice 1 below).
- **"Told apart by poles."** A place pole in a mention is relative to the noun's category of places, as an entity pole is relative to its noun's category (CG.3): "the wide `PLACECATEGORY.1.2`" says `ABOVE(PLACESCALARDIM.1, REF.3, PLACECATEGORY.1.2, 1.0)`. The class is the runtime's, from `comparison_classes`, which holds every place category and `EVERYPLACE` since stage c1.
- **"A place described."** An instance-level sentence with the place as its subject: a place property or part, membership in a category of places (named by another category), or a place pole against a category the place is below. A place is still never the subject of an event, a timed sentence, or a class-level sentence.
- **"A binding that only the compiled `move` literal blocks."** A move whose binding is able and not legal, and legal once the compiled `move` literal is left out: the agent is at the place and nothing else stops the move. A move blocked by another literal too is a candidate, because "the owl could not go to the wet place" is then false for another reason than its being there.
- **"After a sentence that introduces the second of them."** Any narrative sentence that first mentions a place (an event sentence, an initial location state, a result naming a new place) when another place of the document's scenes was mentioned before; the new place is the subject.
- **"The change `object`."** A new change beside `role`: for a pair state between entities or a `SAME` state, another entity referent of the document; for a location state, another place of the scene; for an event of a place event type, another place of the scene. A swap keeps the kind of the second argument, so the two items of a pair have the same format.
- **"`role`, skipped for symmetric pair fluents, for `SAME`, and for locations."** The state sets gain the role swap for pair states on pair fluents between entities that are not symmetric; an asymmetric pair fluent's swap is always false, a general one's may be true (then no item).
- **"The three kinds of false event"** for a place event type follow from the grounding as for any event: `possible` when the binding was legal at some time point of the scene, `blocked` when able and never legal, `impossible` when not able.

## Engineering choices made while building

Each choice is logged in `docs/DECISIONS.md` as the WM.E row named beside it.

1. **The initial states with pairs, places, and `SAME` (WM.E344).** `Planner._initial_state` draws among the Boolean fluents, the numeric poles, and the pair-state predicates of `Facts.pair_predicates`: a pair fluent between entities with a preposition toward each entity of the scene already mentioned, each location fluent with a preposition toward the participant's own place (a positive state) and toward the places of the scene already mentioned (a negative one), and each `SAME` preposition toward each entity already mentioned (ruling 1). The polarity is drawn at `propositions.negation_rate.state` as before, and the predicate uniformly among those with the value at the time point. A negative location state names a place already mentioned, because "was not at a place" would introduce a place by denying something of it; a positive one names the participant's own place, mentioned or not, as the specification's example does ("a mouse was at a dry place"). `Truth.predicate_holds` judges a pair state through `pair_holds_at`, which reads the pair, or for `SAME` whether the two rows of the functional pair fluent share a column (the same place, on a location fluent). A `SAME` state is never a change: `_evaluate_timed` refuses `BECOME` over it, because no one event records a change of `SAME`. The oracle judges a `SAME` state from the replayed pairs. `World.is_same_literal` and `pair_fluent_of` read the `SAME` concepts, and the rendering's `_kind` maps the prefix `SAME.` to `pair_state`.
2. **Place poles as modifiers (WM.E345, ruling 2).** `MentionRules.place_preference` holds the place scalars after the place properties and parts, in the order drawn from `mentions:place_preference`; `value` gives a place pole literal when the place is at the pole relative to the noun's category of places, and `fits` judges a place pole by `Truth.place_pole_mask`, which reads the runtime's comparison classes of places (stage c1) at the language's `z`, as `pole_mask` does for entities. The poles join the preference order rather than a separate pass, so a pole is added when it rules out a place that is still left, like any attribute; the entities' preference order is untouched. The tiny world's two places of a category differ on their one place scalar, so each is `HIGH` or `LOW` within its category at `z = 1.0` (each of two values lies exactly one standard deviation from their mean), and the tiny corpus's definite place mentions are distinguished without any change of the place taxonomy (ruling 4).
3. **A place as the subject (WM.E346, ruling 3).** The grammar admits a place as the subject of an instance-level sentence whose predicate is about a place (a place property or part, a category of places, a place pole, or a comparative on a place scalar with another place as its object) and refuses it as the subject of anything else: an event, a sentence about a time point, a pair state, a capacity, a class-level sentence; and it refuses a place feature said of an entity (`_check_place_subject`). The object of a `SAME` preposition is an entity. `Truth._evaluate_place` judges such a proposition: a place feature from the places' static facts, membership from the place tree, a place pole against a category of places the place is below (or `EVERYPLACE`) at the language's `z`, a comparative by the runtime's `greater` over place indices; `_predicate_problem` accepts place features, place categories, place poles, and place scalars, and an entity subject with a place predicate is invalid. `Facts.place_predicates`, `place_facts`, and `draw_place` give a place's true facts as `instance_facts` and `draw_instance` give an entity's; a membership fact is said with another noun than its category, and a place with no other noun is skipped. The oracle's `_instance` reads a place subject from `places.csv` and the brute-force evaluator, and the place's path from its label. Such a sentence reads as an instance-level sentence about an entity does.
4. **Place descriptions and comparisons of places in narratives (WM.E347).** After an event sentence that mentions a place, at `documents.place_description_rate` (default 0.1), the planner describes one place the sentence mentioned, a newly introduced one first, by a fact drawn from `draw_place`, with the stated feature avoided as a modifier of the subject (section `place_description`, drawn from the propositions stream). After any narrative sentence that first mentions a place (an event sentence, an initial state, or a result), when another place of the document's scenes was mentioned before, at `documents.comparative_rate` from the comparatives stream, the planner compares the new place, as the subject, with a place mentioned before on a place scalar where their values differ, the other place and the scalar drawn uniformly among the pairs that differ, both mentions avoiding the compared scalar's poles (section `place_comparative`). The place comparative follows the introducing sentence at once, so it can stand between an event sentence and its other follow-ups, and occur twice when two places are introduced; the order test of the follow-ups allows it there. The default of `place_description_rate` is half the participants' rate, because a place has fewer facts and its description competes with the follow-up sentences of the same event.
5. **Blocked place events and the move rule (WM.E348, WM.53).** `Planner._blocked` adds, for each entity of the event, the bindings of every place event type with a verb and every place of the scene that a noun names, judged by `Truth.allows` and `legal_at` as the entity bindings are. A negative candidate that is a move whose agent is at the place at that time point (`Truth.at_the_place`, by `place_at` on the move's location fluent, `World.move_location`) is skipped, whatever else blocks the move, so that no blocked sentence says that an agent could not go where it was; the stage's acceptance criterion reads review point 5 so, and a first version that evaluated the precondition without the compiled `move` literal (the literal reading of "only this literal blocks") was dropped with the runtime helper it needed. A positive candidate (could do, and did not) is kept as for entity event types.
6. **The test sets (WM.E349, WM.56).** The change `object` joins `CHANGES` and `test_sets.changes`, last, so the earlier sets keep their order; the levels `state` and `event` take it, `state` the role change too, so the sets `state_role_changed`, `state_role_unchanged`, `state_object_changed`, `state_object_unchanged`, `event_object_possible`, `event_object_blocked`, and `event_object_impossible` join the 46 of stage c4b (53 in all). `candidates` gives a pair state another pair fluent of its kind (a location fluent, a pair fluent between entities, or a `SAME` preposition), another participant, the arguments exchanged for a pair fluent between entities that is not symmetric (never for `SAME`, symmetric by its meaning, or for a location, whose second argument is a place), and another second argument of the same kind; a place event another place verb, another participant, or another place, and no role swap. `_draw_state` draws the pair predicates beside the Boolean fluents and the numeric poles, over the document's other entity referents and the places among its referents, and for the role and object changes among the pair states they apply to; `_draw_event` skips a place event for the role change and takes place events alone for the object change; `_draw_able_now` draws the place verbs with the places among the document's referents. The places an item names are the places among its document's referents (`TestSetBuilder.places_of`), as the entities are the entity referents: an item is a continuation of its document and mentions what the document mentioned, with `the` (a place of the scene the document never mentioned would need `a`, and the item's format would differ). `format_differences` compares two lists of one length entry by entry, because the referents of a location or place event item name an entity and then a place, two notations in one list. `ItemSet.stats` gains `pairs_by_item_kind` for the state sets and `place_event_pairs` for the event and `able_now` sets.
7. **The filters undone (WM.E350).** Listed under "The filters of stages c4a and c4b: undone, and what remains" below.
8. **The statistics and the figures (WM.E351).** `stats.yaml`'s `states` block gains `same_state_sentences`, `blocked_place_sentences`, `blocked_move_sentences`, and `place_description_sentences`, the `comparisons` block `place_comparative_sentences`, and each test set the counts of choice 6; `figures.py`'s `pairs_places` block reads them, with `state_items_by_kind` summed over the state sets, `place_event_pairs` over the event and `able_now` sets, and `place_definite_not_distinguished_share`.
9. **The tests (WM.E352).** Listed under "Test results" below, with the earlier tests that were changed.

## The filters of stages c4a and c4b: undone, and what remains

The c4b proposal lists ten filters, continuing the c4a proposal's nine. What this stage undid, item by item (the c4b numbering):

1. **The event items.** Undone: `_reports` lists a document's place events, so the event sets draw moves and acts, with the change `object` (WM.56).
2. **The referents of an item.** Undone in part: `entity_referents` stays the pool of subjects and of subject swaps (a place is never the subject of an item, as it is never the agent of anything), and `places_of` gives the places among a document's referents as the second arguments of location and place event items and the pool of their object swaps.
3. **The changes of the new propositions.** Undone: `candidates` and `changes_for` give a pair state and a place event their changes.
4. **The blocked sentences and the `able_now` items** range over the place verbs and the scene's (or the document's) places too, with the move rule.
5. **The initial states and the state items** draw among the pair states, locations, and `SAME` states too.
6. **A place described or compared.** Undone in its own way: a place is described by its static features and compared with another place in the two new sections; a place is still never described in an instance-level relative clause (`others_known` excludes places), never compared with an entity, and never the subject of a comparative of participants.
7. **A place introduced by an event sentence takes no initial-state sentence.** Stands: a place has no fluent, and its static features are stated by a place description instead.
8. **The causal statements** stand as stage c4a left them, for stage c5a (the c4a proposal's items 4 to 6 and 8).
9. **`stated_propositions`** records a place mention's noun, features, and now poles; the `seen` mark reads them for the state items about places as for entities (a state item is about the final time point, which no document states, so none is seen).
10. **A place event whose place no noun can name** is still skipped, by the narrative and by the test sets (`places_of` keeps the places a noun names).

What remains filtered, and why: a place takes no relative clause, no pronoun, and no modifier at `mention.modifier_rate` (stage c4b, the specification's limits); a `SAME` state is never a change (choice 1); a place is never the subject of an event, a timed sentence, or a class-level sentence (choice 3); the object change applies to pair, location, and `SAME` states and to place events alone, and the role change of a pair state to a pair fluent between entities that is not symmetric (WM.56); and the class level of phase (c) (stages c5a and c5b): `SOMEPLACE`, `them`, the causal and profile statements with pair literals, the documents about pair fluents and places, and the causal, profile, and class sets.

## Open questions

1. **The undistinguished mentions of a comparison of places.** A comparison of two places avoids the compared scalar's poles as modifiers, as a comparative of participants does, so in a world whose places differ within a category by their one scalar alone (the tiny world) the two mentions of "the `PLACECATEGORY.2` is narrower than the `PLACECATEGORY.2`" are not distinguished, and counted so. The sentence is true and its logical form names both places; a language could instead let the compared pole stand as a modifier ("the narrow place is narrower than the wide place"), which says the comparison twice. The same holds of entity comparatives on a one-scalar world. Should the comparison of places (and of participants) allow the compared scalar's poles when nothing else tells the two apart?
2. **A blocked sentence about a move names a place with `a`.** As a blocked sentence about a two-place event type can name a participant not yet mentioned ("the owl could not eat a mouse"), one about a move can name a place of the scene not yet mentioned ("the owl could not go to a wet place"), which introduces the place. Should blocked sentences about places (or about participants) be limited to the referents already mentioned?
3. **The default of `documents.place_description_rate`.** 0.1 is Claude Code's choice (choice 4); the figures of the default corpus below show what it gives. Jon may want it at the participants' 0.2, or at 0.
