### How the pieces stand today

Main holds stage c4a (542561a, the merge of PR #37). The corpus runs on `tiny.yaml` and `default.yaml` with their pair fluents, their location fluent, and their places, and says nothing about them: `World.event_names` gives a place event type no name, the planner skips a place event in `_narrative` and in the earlier events a relative clause can report, `Planner._result` skips pair changes, and the lexicon has no concept of a phase (c) label. The oracle replays the pairs and the places and exposes `pairs_at`, `place_at`, and `pair_holds`. The c4a proposal lists nine skips and filters; this stage takes up the first three (`event_names`, the narrative's two `event.place` checks, and `_result`), and leaves the causal ones (4 to 6, 8) to stage c5a and the statistics one (7) to this stage's ruling 3.

The lexicon's concepts come from `world_concepts` in the order of the specification's table, with `THING` last; the lexemes are numbered in that order, content first, then function words. A mention is a `NounPhrase` of the kind `instance` with a determiner `a` or `the`, a noun, a restriction of `Literal`s, and at most one relative clause; `Mentions` numbers the referents of a document by first mention and tells them apart by the incremental algorithm over a preference order drawn once per language. The grammar realizes a comparative's standard as a `PP` of `than` and the object noun phrase, placed where a verb's object stands, with `than` before or after the noun phrase by `word_order.adposition`: the model for a prepositional predicate. The timed comparative of stage b3b carries its patient in a timed predication: the model for a pair state. The word-form pipeline reads a lexeme's part of speech as a string and looks up its affixes in the request's `takes`, so a part of speech it has never seen (a preposition) needs no change there.

### Plan for the stage

In the order of the scope:

1. **The lexicon.** New concept types in `config.CONCEPT_TYPES` and `lexicon.named_proportion`: `pair` and `same` (prepositions), `place_category` (nouns), `place_is` and `place_has` (adjectives and part nouns), `place_scalar` (two pole adjectives), `place_event` (transitive verbs), and `place_patient_projection` (adjectives, a quarter by default); a new part of speech, `preposition`; the concepts appended after `THING`, so that every existing content lexeme keeps its label.
2. **The grammar.** The predicate kind `pair_state` in `Predication`, realized as the copula (or `become`) and a `PP` of the preposition and the object noun phrase, the preposition before or after by `word_order.adposition`, the `PP` placed where an object stands; the predicate kind `place_event_type` for an event of a place event type, a transitive verb with a place mention as its object; `interpret` and the readings extended; `check_plan` keeps the limits.
3. **Places as referents.** `Mentions` numbers places with the entities, introduces a place with `a`, mentions it later with `the`, names it at a level of the place tree by `mention.place_level_weights`, tells it apart from the document's scenes' other places by place properties and parts, never by a pronoun, and gives it no relative clause.
4. **The predicate kind `pair_state`.** The `Predicate`, its JSON form, the propositional rendering (`HOLDS(..., PAIRFL.2(REF.1, REF.2))`, `BECOME(..., NOT PAIRFL.5(REF.1, REF.3))`), its reading, and its truth from the replayed pairs; the oracle judges it from `pair_holds`.
5. **Moves and acts in narratives.** `World.event_names` names a place event type with dynamics; the planner's two `event.place` checks go; `Truth._evaluate_event` and the oracle accept a place as the patient.
6. **Result sentences.** `Planner._result` offers the event's own pair changes: a pair between participants, a move's new place, and the loss of the old place, each with `become`.
7. **The filters.** Listed below under "What the corpus skips or filters".
8. **The statistics.** `stats.yaml` and `figures.py`.
9. **The runs.** The reference run first (made from main before any change, into `semantic-world-reference/c4b/`); at the end the tiny and default worlds (identities unchanged) and corpora regenerated, the word forms remade with `all` from the new requests, both corpora rendered, the snapshot and the guides' figures rewritten.

### Reading of the scope

- **"That some precondition or condition reads."** A pair fluent gets a word when some precondition literal or condition literal of some event type of the world is a `pair` literal on it (`PAIRFL.k(x, y)`, compiled literals included: a move's `NOT PAIRFL.5(agent, patient)` and an act's `PAIRFL.5(agent, patient)` read the location fluent); a `SAME` literal `SAME.PAIRFL.k` gets a word when some precondition or condition reads it. `SOME` and `SOMEOF` get no word (WM.55, stage c5a). The `SAME` words are made in this stage, as the scope says, and used by no sentence of this stage: the statements with `SAME` are stage c5a's, and the instance level states no `SAME` (note 1 for stage c4c).
- **"A quarter of the place patient capacities."** `named_proportion.place_patient_projection: 0.25`, drawn from the lexicon stream's own part, as the entity patient capacities are. The words exist in this stage and are used by no sentence (the capacity facts about places are stage c5b's).
- **"Place scalars (two pole adjectives)."** The words are made; a place pole is used by no sentence of this stage, because comparative descriptions of places are stage c4c's, and the specification and the stage prompt tell places apart "by static place features (properties and parts)". Note 2 for stage c4c: whether a place pole tells places apart, as an entity pole does.
- **"Told apart from the scene's other places."** The distractors of a place mention are the places of the document's scenes, as the distractors of an entity mention are the participants of the document's scenes (CG.E54).
- **"A change is said with become."** A result sentence states a pair change as `BECOME(SCENE, TIME, PAST, PAIRFL.k(REF.a, REF.b))` or with `NOT` for a deletion, the loss of the old place included ("the owl became not at the wet place"); the polarity inside says the new value, as it does for a Boolean change.
- **"No pronoun refers to a place."** A place mention is never `it`; the subject of a sentence about a pair may be a pronoun when it is an entity, by the usual rule.
- **"The test sets must not take this stage's sentences."** Every set's draws stay over the entities and the entity event types: the filters are listed below. A test item never mentions a place in this stage.

## Engineering choices made while building

Each choice is logged in `docs/DECISIONS.md` as the WM.E row named beside it.

1. **The concepts of phase (c) come after the generic noun (WM.E335).** `world_concepts` appends them after `THING`, in the order of the specification's table (`pair`, `same`, `place_category`, `place_is`, `place_has`, `place_scalar`, `place_event`, `place_patient_projection`), so that every content lexeme of the entities' concepts, `THING` included, keeps its label from stage c4a; only the function words' labels shift. The new concept types join `CONCEPT_TYPES` and `lexicon.named_proportion` with the proportion 1.0, `place_patient_projection` with 0.25 (the two poles of a place scalar named together, as a scalar's are). A pair fluent gets a preposition when some precondition or condition literal of some event type reads it as a pair (`World.pair_fluents_read`: a `pair` literal, compiled literals included, so a move's `NOT PAIRFL.5(agent, patient)` names the location fluent), and `SAME.PAIRFL.k` when some literal reads `SAME` on it (`same_read`). A place event type gets a verb when it has dynamics (a move or an act, whose events a narrative reports) and its requirement holds for some binding and not every binding, by `relation_extent` over the bindings of an entity and a place; a place event type without dynamics has no events and no word in this stage, so a world with places and no location fluent (the earlier tests' written worlds) names its categories of places, its place features, and its place scalars, and no place event type. The prepositions are the new part of speech `preposition`, a content lexeme that takes no affix: the request's `takes` has no entry for it, and the word-form pipeline, which reads a lexeme's part of speech as a string and looks its affixes up in `takes`, needs no change (note 5 of stage b3b, confirmed).
2. **The predicate kinds `pair_state` and `place_event_type` (WM.E336).** A pair state is a `Predicate` of the kind `pair_state` with the pair fluent as its `label` and the second argument, an entity or a place, as its `patient`, at the levels `state` and `change`; its JSON form is the timed form with the patient a mention (`"patient": {"instance": "PLACE.1.2.1", ...}`), and its rendering `HOLDS(SCENE.8, TIME.2, PAST, PAIRFL.2(REF.1, REF.2))` or `BECOME(..., NOT PAIRFL.5(REF.1, REF.3))`, the value negated inside as a Boolean fluent's is. An event of a place event type is a `Predicate` of the kind `place_event_type` (the world's symbol kind, beside `event_type1` and `event_type2`) with the place as its `patient`, rendered `EVENT(SCENE.8.EVENTINSTANCE.4, PAST, SIMPLE, PLACEEVENTTYPE.2(REF.1, REF.3))`; the kind is its own, rather than `event_type2`, so that every reader that treats a two-place event type's patient as an entity (the relation facts, the test sets, the able tables) is untouched, and the propositional rendering reads an atom's kind from its label's prefix as before. The truth test of a pair state reads the replayed pairs (`Truth.pair_value`, from `State.pairs`) as `fluent_value` reads a Boolean fluent; a change's grounding names the event of the step that recorded the pair change (`pair_caused_by`, either direction for a symmetric pair fluent). The oracle judges both from `pair_holds` and `matching`.
3. **A mention of a place is a noun phrase of the kind `instance` (WM.E337).** `NounPhrase.place` tells one by its referent's label (`PLACE.`), so the JSON mention keeps its shape (`instance`, `referent`, `noun`, `restriction`, `descriptive`), the referent labels number entities and places together by first mention (`REF.3` for a place in the specification's example), and `interpret` reads a place mention as it reads an entity's. A place mention is named at a level of the place tree by `mention.place_level_weights` (a new setting, the linear schedule from 1 to 4 over the place tree's depth by default, as `level_weights` is for entities), told apart from the other places of the document's scenes by place properties and parts in a preference order drawn once per language from the part `mentions:place_preference` of the mentions stream (so the entities' preference order is unchanged), kept and counted as not distinguished when they cannot (CG.43, the leaf noun tried first as for entities), and never a pronoun, never with a relative clause, never with a modifier added at `mention.modifier_rate`, and never the subject of a sentence. The place poles are not used to tell places apart in this stage (the specification and the stage prompt say "properties and parts"); note 2 for stage c4c. The grammar refuses a pair fluent or a `SAME` literal as a modifier of any mention, a pair state in a relative clause, a place feature on an entity's mention and an entity feature on a place's, and an entity as the object of a place event type.
4. **The prepositional phrase stands where an object stands (WM.E338).** The realizer makes a pair state as the copula (or `become`, or `is not` and `become not` for a negative value) with no predicate word of its own, and a `PP` of the preposition (a `P` node with a content lexeme) and the object noun phrase, the preposition before or after the noun phrase by `word_order.adposition`; the `PP` is the verb phrase's target, placed as a verb's object is (inside the verb phrase when the verb and the object are neighbors, a daughter of the sentence when the subject stands between them), as the `than` phrase of a comparative is. `interpret` finds the object of a pair state as it finds a comparative's standard, in a `PP` under the sentence or the verb phrase whose adposition's lexeme is a preposition, and reads a verb phrase with no verb, adjective, or predicate noun and such a `PP` as a pair state at the time point the record gives (with a category as the subject it refuses, stage c5a). The readings read such a verb phrase as `state`. A place event type is a transitive verb whose object is a place mention, realized and read as a two-place event type's is, with the prefix `PLACEEVENTTYPE.` giving its kind.
5. **Moves and acts in narratives (WM.E339).** `World.event_names` names a place event type with dynamics by its own label (the place event types are flat), which turns on `happened`, the event-level forms, and the relative clauses that report earlier events; `Facts.event_fact` gives an event of a place event type the kind `place_event_type` with the place as its patient, and `draw_event` draws no level for it. The planner's two `event.place` checks are gone: a narrative reports a move or an act in its turn, with the place introduced with `a` and mentioned later with `the`, and a relative clause of an entity's mention can report an earlier move or act ("the owl that went to the dry place"), its place a mention too. An event whose place no noun can name (a category of places without a word) is skipped, as an event no word reports is. `Truth._evaluate_event` and `legal_in`, `legal_at`, and `allows` take a place as the patient of a place event type (the place's column of the place able table; the legal table of a time point holds the place event types' bindings over the scene's places), and the event's grounding has `able` and `legal` as any event's.
6. **Result sentences with pairs and places (WM.E340, WM.72).** `Planner._result` adds, beside the Boolean, numeric, and derived candidates, every pair change of the event's own recorded changes whose pair fluent has a preposition: for a pair fluent between entities the recorded direction (a symmetric pair once, as the history records it), for a location fluent the move's new place (`to` true) and the loss of the old place (`to` false), each as `Predicate(pair_state, fluent, second)` at the level `change` with the recorded value as its polarity, drawn uniformly with the others; the second argument is an entity of the scene or a place of the scene, so the mention rules name it. A place event's patient is never a candidate for a derived-fluent change, a blocked sentence, a comparative description, or an instance description, and a place introduced by an event sentence takes no initial-state sentence (the initial states with pairs and places are stage c4c's).
7. **The filters of this stage (WM.E341).** Listed under "What the corpus skips or filters" below.
8. **The statistics (WM.E342).** `stats.yaml`'s `states` block gains `pair_state_sentences` (HOLDS with a pair fluent, 0 in this stage), `pair_change_sentences` (BECOME with a pair fluent), `location_sentences` with `location_state_sentences` and `location_change_sentences` (those on a location fluent), and `move_sentences` and `act_sentences` (the narrated events of place event types, by `place_kind`); the `scenes` block gains `place_events`, `moves`, `acts`, `move_share`, `act_share`, `pair_changes` (a move's two changes counted as one move, Jon's ruling 3), `pair_changes_between_entities`, and `pair_changes_per_event`; the `mentions` block gains `place_mentions`, `place_indefinite`, `place_definite`, `place_definite_not_distinguished`, and its share; and `lexicon.lexemes_by_pos` gains `preposition`. `figures.py` reads them into a `pairs_places` block of the corpus figures.
9. **The oracle (WM.E343).** `tests/corpus/truth_oracle.py` judges a `pair_state` form by `pair_holds` at the time point (a state) and at the time point and the next (a change), an event form with a place event type by `matching` as any event (the patient a place label, the names of a flat place event type its own label), and `made_by` of a pair fluent by the event's recorded pair changes, either direction for a symmetric pair fluent. Nothing of the corpus's code is shared.

## What the corpus skips or filters

Every place where the corpus steps around pairs and places in this stage, so that stage c4c can take them up one by one (the numbering continues the c4a proposal's list, whose items 4 to 6 and 8 stand, for stage c5a):

1. **The event items.** `TestSetBuilder._reports` lists a document's reported events without the place events, so no event item, true or false, is a move or an act; the change `object` of the event sets of place event types is stage c4c's (WM.56).
2. **The referents of an item.** `TestSetBuilder.entity_referents` gives the entities among a document's referents: what an instance, instance-comparison, state, state-comparison, or `able_now` item is about, and what a subject swap can bring in; a place is never mentioned by an item.
3. **The changes of the new propositions.** `candidates` and `changes_for` give a `pair_state` or `place_event_type` proposition no change, so `falsify` never makes a false item from one.
4. **The blocked sentences and the `able_now` items** range over `facts.features[CAN]` and `facts.verbs`; the place verbs are kept apart in `facts.place_verbs`, so no sentence says what a participant could or could not do at a place (the move rule, review point 5, is stage c4c's).
5. **The initial states and the state items** draw among the Boolean fluents with words and the numeric poles with words; a pair state at a time point (`HOLDS` over a pair fluent) is built, rendered, read, and judged, and no sentence of this stage makes one.
6. **A place is never described or compared:** `_description` takes the entities of the event, `_comparative` skips a place event, and an instance-level relative clause relates a referent to the entities mentioned so far (`others_known`), never to a place.
7. **A place introduced by an event sentence takes no initial-state sentence** (`introduced` lists the entities).
8. **The causal statements** are as stage c4a left them: `condition_of`, `effect_matches`, and `has_entry` refuse pair literals, the enumeration runs over the entity event types, and the test of the enumeration skips the place event types, which have words now and no causal statement until stage c5a.
9. **`stated_propositions`** records a place mention's noun and place features as it records an entity's, for `seen`; no item of this stage looks one up.
10. **A place event whose place no noun can name** (a category of places without a word) is skipped by the narrative, as an event no word reports is; with the default proportions every category of places has a noun.

## Open questions

1. **An instance-level `SAME` sentence.** The `SAME` prepositions exist from this stage (the scope says so) and no sentence uses one before the causal statements of stage c5a. "The owl was with the mouse" (at the same place as) is a pair state of a derived pair fluent, `HOLDS(SCENE.8, TIME.2, PAST, SAME.PAIRFL.5(REF.1, REF.2))`, which the runtime can judge from the replayed places. Should stage c4c state it, as an initial state and in the state sets, or does `SAME` stay a class-level word?
2. **Place poles as modifiers.** Entities are told apart by properties, parts, and scalar poles; this stage tells places apart by properties and parts, as the specification and the prompt say, so a place pole adjective has a word and no use until the comparative descriptions of stage c4c. Should c4c let a place pole tell places apart (the default world's 106 undistinguished place mentions would fall)?
3. **A place described.** Narratives describe participants at `documents.instance_description_rate` ("it can hunt a mouse"); a place has static features that an instance-level sentence could state ("the place is dry"), which no stage of phase (c) names. Is a description of a place wanted, and in which stage?
4. **The tiny place taxonomy.** Its two places of a category share every place property, so no definite place mention of the tiny corpus is distinguished (3 of 3). The tiny world's identity may not move in this stage; a tiny place taxonomy whose places differ within a category would need a change of `data/taxonomy/places_tiny.yaml` and a new tiny identity, which a later stage could make.

## Notes for stage c4c

- The predicate kind `pair_state` at the level `state` is built end to end (JSON, rendering, `interpret`, readings, `Truth._evaluate_pair_state` with a `changed` grounding against `TIME.1`, and the oracle's `state`), and the planner's `_initial_state` is the one place to offer it: the participant's place on each location fluent, and its pairs with the referents already mentioned, as the specification says.
- The blocked events of place event types need `Truth.legal_at` with a place patient (built: the legal table holds the place event types' bindings over the scene's places) and the move rule of review point 5: a binding that only the compiled `move` literal blocks is the agent's own place, which `Truth.pair_value(scene, time, location, agent, place)` tells.
- The comparative descriptions of places need `Truth.compare` on place scalars, which the runtime supports since stage c1 (`comparison_classes` holds `EVERYPLACE` and the place categories), and a `comparative` predicate between two places; `Described.fits` of the tests and the oracle's `pole` would need place scalars.
- The test sets with pairs and places: `candidates` returns no change for `pair_state` and `place_event_type` (filter 3), `_reports` leaves out the place events (filter 1), and `entity_referents` the places (filter 2); the change `object` (WM.56) belongs beside `ROLE` in `CHANGES` and `LEVEL_CHANGES`, and `format_differences` must accept a place mention against an entity mention in one pair only where the change makes it (an `object` swap keeps the kind of the second argument).
- The place mentions of a test item come from `Mentions.place_phrase` with the item's own stream part, as entity mentions do; the places of a document are `scene.places` of its scenes.
- `stats.yaml` counts the pair states (`pair_state_sentences`, 0 now) and the location states already.
- The tiny word-form configuration (`data/wordforms/corpus_tiny.yaml`) makes 60 content words for a corpus that now has 57 content lexemes; stage c5a's `SOMEPLACE` takes one more, and a stage that adds further content lexemes to the tiny corpus raises `wordforms.count` there (the pipeline stops when the words are fewer than the lexemes).
