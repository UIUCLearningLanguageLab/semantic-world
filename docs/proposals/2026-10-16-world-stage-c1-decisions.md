### Reading of the scope

- **"Every real the world draws and stores at full precision."** The taxonomy's scalar values (drawn from normal distributions by the taxonomy, written to `base.csv` at 6 decimals and copied into `entities.csv` at full precision by WM.E33) are the taxonomy's draws, not the world's, and the stage requires every taxonomy output to stay byte-identical; they are left alone and listed under "checked" below, with the remark that a normal draw could in principle show the same cross-machine difference.
- **"Places are not entities."** The place taxonomy run is a `TaxonomyResult` of its own on the `WorldResult`, never merged into the entity side: `ENTITY`, `entities.csv`, `capacities.csv`'s rows, the episodes, and the corpus's `World` see entities only; places get their own table, their own comparison-class root, and their own scopes in the definition.
- **"In this stage place event types have no preconditions or effects."** A place event type's record in `definition.json` carries an empty precondition and no effects; the runtime's `able` and `legal_bindings` accept it over (entity, place) bindings, `legal` equals `able`, and `apply` refuses an event of a place event type until stage c3 gives them dynamics. Episodes list only event types of kind `event_type` as performable, so no episode includes one.
- **"Over pairs of an entity category and a place category at the same tree depth."** The rule chosen is recorded in choice 13 below.
- **"The code's default follows `data/world/default.yaml`."** A world configuration without a `places` block gets the default place taxonomy, as a world without a `taxonomy` block gets `relations.yaml` (WM.E41); `places: null` turns places off.

## Item 1: the rounding of drawn reals (WM.60)

Every real the world draws and stores was found by reading every `rng.uniform`, `rng.beta`, `rng.normal`, and `rng.random` call of the world package and following each drawn value to where it is stored.

**Rounded when drawn, to 4 decimals** (`numeric.DECIMALS`, `numeric.drawn`):

- A numeric fluent's drift (`numeric.py`, `generate_numeric_fluents`, from `world:numeric`): 0.072826 became 0.0728 and 0.005553 became 0.0056 in both worlds.
- Each entity's initial value of a numeric fluent, uniform or beta (`InitialDistribution.draw`, from `world:numeric_initial` when the world is generated, and from an episode's part of `world:episodes` under `scene.initial: redraw`), rounded and then clamped to the range, because a rounded value can cross a bound given to more than 4 decimals. A fixed initial value is the configuration's own number.
- A numeric effect's constant (`event_types.py`, `_numeric_effect`, from `world:numeric_effects`): `0.3640982468028209` became `0.3641` in the default world's inherited conditional effect.

**Checked and left alone**, with the reason:

- Scalar thresholds of the taxonomy's rules, the one-place requirements, the constraints, and the derived fluents' rules: already rounded to 4 decimals when drawn (`Threshold.__post_init__`, `model_quantile_threshold`, REL.9).
- Comparison margins: already rounded (`Cmp.__post_init__`, `_comparison`).
- Numeric threshold points of precondition, condition, and rule literals (`NumericFluent.point`): not drawn; computed from the configured range and a fraction drawn from a list (`numeric_thresholds`), exact under the default range `[0, 1]`, and the corpus computes the same expression for the language's cut, so rounding it here would break that equality for an odd range. Noted as a remark below.
- Initial rates of Boolean fluents, and the fractions of numeric thresholds: drawn from lists of configured numbers.
- The taxonomy's scalar values (normal draws, written to `base.csv` at 6 decimals and copied into `entities.csv` at full precision by WM.E33) and base rates (beta draws): the taxonomy's draws, which the stage requires to stay byte-identical (the golden hashes). Remark: a normal draw could in principle show the same cross-machine difference; nothing of the kind was reported.
- The local test's samples of the projections, the sampled pairs of the relation statistics, and the episodes' draws of participants and events: not stored as reals.

The tiny world's generated fixtures were regenerated (`make-fixtures`); the hand-written fixtures use multiples of a quarter and did not change (`python tests/world/hand_world.py` rewrote them byte for byte). `tests/world/test_world_rounding.py` checks that every stored real of the tiny and default worlds, on seeds 1 to 5, equals itself rounded, in the objects and in the definition's record; that a redrawn initial state draws rounded values; and that a fixed value is the configuration's.

**Figures moved by item 1 alone** (the b3b snapshot against the worlds and corpora with `places: null` after the rounding; the identities first):

- `tiny.world.rule_set_id`: `912f1d371460...` became `6834c215f732...`; `default.world.rule_set_id`: `f47334502194...` became `cc1e27fac819...`. These are the `places_off` baselines of the new snapshot.
- `tiny.world.numeric.drifts` and `default.world.numeric.drifts`: `[0.072826, 0.005553]` became `[0.0728, 0.0056]` (the figures are rounded to six decimals; the stored values moved from 0.07282598... and 0.00555313... to the four-decimal values).
- `default.world.conditional.expressions`, `.inherited`, and `.own`: the numeric constants of the conditional effects, four decimals now (for example `patient.NUMFL.1 += 0.3641`).
- `default.world.numeric.changes_per_event`: 0.40818 became 0.4083; `default.world.numeric.drift_changes_per_step`: 8.427322 became 8.425501.
- The default corpus (the tiny corpus's figures did not move apart from the identity): `sentences` 100,917 became 100,918; `tokens` 483,329 became 483,322; `by_level.change` 9,608 became 9,610 and `by_level.event` 28,964 became 28,963; `comparisons.pole_modifiers` 2,972 became 2,969; `states.events_with_result_share` 0.331722 became 0.331803, `states.result_sentences` 9,608 became 9,610, `states.numeric_change_sentences` 3,791 became 3,794; `cooccurrence.words.situational.taxonomic` 0.206033 became 0.206018 and `.thematic` 0.632423 became 0.63219; `corpus.world` moved with the identity.

## Engineering choices made while building

Numbered for `docs/DECISIONS.md` (WM.E240 to WM.E255). Each is Claude Code's choice inside the stage's scope.

1. **The rounding** (above): `DECIMALS = 4` and `drawn()` in `numeric.py`, applied to the drift, the numeric effect constants, and the initial values (rounded, then clamped); the quantities checked and left alone are listed above.
2. **`labels.kind`.** A `Labels` record (the category, instance, property, part, and scalar prefixes) on the taxonomy configuration, read from `labels.kind: entity | place` (default `entity`) and carried by `build_features` (the feature labels and `FeatureSet.scalar_prefix`), the tree settings (the category prefix), the instances (the instance prefix), and `Threshold.prefix` (a threshold literal's scalar label, drawn or explicit); the ISA labels follow the category labels. `resolved()` writes `labels: {kind}` after `seed`, and `data/taxonomy/default.yaml` lists it. Nothing else changes: `tests/taxonomy/test_taxonomy_labels.py` checks that a place run equals the entity run of the same settings label for label (the YAML files compared as data, because the longer labels wrap lines elsewhere), that the ISA columns, thresholds, and explicit rules use the place labels, and the golden hashes of the entity configurations did not move. The golden hashes gained the two place configurations.
3. **The place taxonomies as built.** `places_default.yaml`: 2 superordinates, depth 2, branching 3, 2 places per leaf (12 places in 8 categories), 12 place properties with a quarter determined and `expected_true_free: 3` of the 9 free (a free place property is true of a third of the places on average; the entity default of 6 would be two thirds of 9), no parts (`part: {count: 0}`), 2 scalars, the taxonomy's defaults otherwise. `places_tiny.yaml`: 2 superordinates at depth 1 (the superordinates are the leaves, which the taxonomy allows) with 2 places each (4 places in 2 categories), 4 place properties with 1 determined and `expected_true_free: 1` of the 3 free, no parts, 1 scalar, and `base_rate_heterogeneity: null` as WM.E108 set it for the tiny entity taxonomies, so that the similarity bound can place the superordinates. Both carry `labels.kind: place`.
4. **The `places` block.** `PlacesConfig(taxonomy, habitat_strength, taxonomy_run)`: the named place taxonomy configuration is validated at configuration time with its seed resolved, the given seed or the low 64 bits of the seed of the stream `world:places` (the taxonomy's seeds are 64-bit integers, the stream's seed a 256-bit digest), and must set `labels.kind: place`. A world configuration without a `places` key gets `data/taxonomy/places_default.yaml` (the code's default follows `data/world/default.yaml`, as WM.E41 did for the entity taxonomy), and `places: null` turns places off. `place_event_types` (`count` 4, `move_share` 0.5, `constraints` `{1: 0.6, 2: 0.4}` with no weight on 0, `constraint_families` `{agent: 1, patient: 1, cross: 1, key_lock: 1}`, `rules` as overrides of the entity taxonomy's rule settings) is `None` exactly when places are off, and giving the block with `places: null` is an error; `move_share` and `habitat_strength` are validated and stored for stage c3. The comparison family needs scalars in both taxonomies. The key-lock pairs and the comparison settings of the place constraints are those of `event_types.binary` (the code's defaults when the world has no two-place event types), rather than new keys the specification does not list. The resolved configuration writes `places` (with the resolved place taxonomy under `places.taxonomy.resolved`) and `place_event_types` after `event_types`.
5. **`places.py` and `WorldResult.places`.** `Places` holds the place taxonomy run, the place event types, each requirement over every binding as an `(entities, places)` bool matrix, and the capacities; `generate_places` makes it after the static side (its streams are its own, so the order does not matter). `write` writes the run unmodified to `places/` and the places' base facts to `places.csv` (label, leaf, free place features, place scalars at full precision, as `entities.csv` holds the entities' base facts). Nothing of it enters `StaticWorld`, `entities.csv`, `capacities.csv`'s rows, the episodes, or the corpus's `World`.
6. **The constraint sampler over two pools.** `_ConstraintSampler` takes an optional patient pool (the place taxonomy's scalars and features): the patient's literals come from it, a patient threshold carries the place scalar prefix, and a comparison compares an entity scalar with a place scalar, always across dimensions, with the model standard deviation of the difference `sqrt(entity_std^2 + place_std^2)` (`ScalarComparison.patient_prefix`, `agent_label`, `patient_label`; `Constraint.matrix` takes the patients' tables). With one pool the sampler's draws are unchanged, so the two-place event types of a world are those of stage b3b (the `places_off` baseline checks it).
7. **Place event types and their redraw.** `PLACEEVENTTYPE.<n>` draws the number of its constraints from `place_event_types.constraints`, labels them `CONSTRAINT.PLACEEVENTTYPE.<n>.<k>`, and ANDs them as `REQUIREMENT.PLACEEVENTTYPE.<n>`. A requirement that holds for no binding or for every binding of the world is drawn again, up to 200 times (the two-place `max_tries` default), after which the last draw is kept with a warning in `world_stats.yaml`. The check is over the world's bindings (every entity paired with every place), because the specification says "of the world"; the two-place check is over leaf pairs. The draws, and the samples of the capacities' local test, come from `world:place_event_types`.
8. **The definition.** With places, `definition.json` gains the symbol kinds `place_property`, `place_part`, `place_scalar`, and `place_event_type`; the rule and layer scopes `place` (the determined place features, evaluated over the places) and `place_binding` (the place constraints and requirements, evaluated over bindings of an entity and a place); place literals after the entity literals in the literal table, in the same group order (a place feature or threshold of the place scope, a place feature or threshold of the patient, a comparison with a place scalar, a place constraint's output), while the agent's literals (`agent.PROPERTY.3`) are shared with the binding scope; and a place event type's record with kind `place_event_type`, arity 2, the roles agent and patient, no parent, level 1, no features, `explicit: false`, an empty precondition (`TRUE`), and no effects. The agreement test (`check_definition`, `DefinitionReport.place` and `.place_binding`) covers every place rule on every place and every place constraint and requirement on every entity paired with every place, against the constraints' own evaluation. With `places: null` nothing of this is written, so the definition and its identity are those of the world before places.
9. **The runtime.** `RuntimeDefinition` loads `places.csv` beside `entities.csv` (required when the record has place symbols, refused otherwise, with the columns checked as the entities' are) into the places' labels, leaves, free values, and scalar values, with the rules and matrices of both place scopes. `EventTypeRecord.place` (kind `place_event_type`) and `EventTypeRecord.performable` (kind `event_type`) tell the kinds apart; the episodes, the fixtures, and the brute-force evaluator list the performable event types only. `able` and `legal` accept a place event type over bindings of an entity index and a place index (`place_able_table`, cached), `legal_bindings(definition, state, event_type, entities, places)` lists them by agent then place, `check_definition_agreement` covers the place scopes, and `apply` refuses an event of a place event type with a `WorldError`, because stage c1 gives them no dynamics (stage c3 lifts this).
10. **Comparisons over places.** `EVERYPLACE` and the place categories are comparison classes on the place scalars (`comparison_classes`, `members`, `place_categories`), an argument on a place scalar is a place index or `Mean` of a class of places, and a comparison that mixes an entity scalar or a numeric fluent with a class of places, or a place scalar with a class of entities, is refused with a `WorldError` (`_check_class_of_dimension`). `derived/comparison_classes.csv` holds the place rows after the entity rows: `EVERYPLACE` first, then the place tree in tree order, on every place scalar.
11. **Capacities.** `project` is reused with the unknown side's rules apart from the known side's instances: `CAN.PLACEEVENTTYPE.<n>` enumerates the place taxonomy's cones for the entities, `CANBE.PLACEEVENTTYPE.<n>` the entity taxonomy's cones for the places, each with the local test's approximate flag, then the extensional capacities over the world's bindings. In `capacities.csv` the `CAN.PLACEEVENTTYPE` columns follow the two-place `CANBE` columns before `approximate` (which names approximate place columns too), and the `ACTUAL_CAN.PLACEEVENTTYPE` columns come last, so every earlier column keeps its place; `derived/place_capacities.csv` holds one row per place with `CANBE.`, `approximate`, and `ACTUAL_CANBE.` columns.
12. **Relation statistics.** `relation_proportions.csv` gains rows for the place event types: for each level L of the entity tree, every entity category at level L against every place category at level L, or at the place tree's last level when the entity tree is deeper (the default world's entity leaves at level 3 against the place leaves at level 2), with the share of their bindings for which the requirement holds, rows with a share above 0. `event_type_stats.csv` gains one row per place event type with the share of all bindings, the entities and places with a partner, the constraints and families, NaN for the symmetric and within-leaf shares (a binding is never reversed, and an entity shares no leaf with a place), and the binding density under `leaf_pair_density` with its tries. Both files are written when the world has places even without two-place event types; `relation_pairs.csv` and `thematic.csv` are unchanged, and thematic relatedness stays a relation between entity leaves.
13. **Statistics and figures.** `world_stats.yaml` gains a `places` block (places, categories, place features by kind with free and determined counts, place event types, constraints by family, each requirement's density over the world's bindings and its tries, the share of approximate capacities, and warnings), null without places. `figures.py` reads it into a `places` block of the world figures, keeps the two-place requirement figure over the two-place rows of `event_type_stats.csv` alone, names the places and place event types in the `define` line and the quick-start figure, and adds `places_off` (`places: null`, `place_event_types: null`) to the off settings with its baseline identities.
14. **Fixtures.** A fixture of a world with places carries `places` (the rows of `places.csv`) after `entities`; `check_fixture` and the brute-force evaluator read it (`BruteForce(record, entities, places)`, with `_row` finding an entity or a place by label), a place event type's bindings in the evaluator are every entity paired with every place, and a step's `legal` lists the performable event types only. The regenerated tiny fixtures carry the places and the place scopes. `tests/fixtures/world/README.md` gains a "Places" section for the Rust runtime.
15. **`view --places`.** `RUN/views/places_<preset>.csv`: `base` (label, leaf, free place features, place scalars), `static` (with the determined place features and the `CANBE.PLACEEVENTTYPE` capacities), and `classic` (with the ISA columns of the place tree); the sidecar records `places: true`, `--include` works over the place columns, and the entities' views never show a place column.
16. **The corpus, checked.** With places on, the tiny corpus's `documents.jsonl`, `lexicon.csv`, `stats.yaml`, the four renderings, the test sets, and the word-form request are byte-identical to the corpus over the same world with `places: null`, and `scenes.jsonl` differs in the rule-set identity alone; no corpus output names a place, a place feature, or a place event type (`grep PLACE` finds nothing). The default corpus's figures moved only by item 1 (above): the snapshot's corpus figures are the same with and without places apart from the world's identity. The word forms were not remade, because both corpora's word-form requests are byte-identical to stage b3b's; `render` attached the b3b word-form runs.

## Open questions

None of these changes the design; each is recorded for Jon's eye.

1. **The taxonomy's scalar values at full precision.** `entities.csv` copies the entities' scalar values at full precision (WM.E33), and `places.csv` the places'. They are the taxonomy's normal draws, left alone because the taxonomy's outputs must stay byte-identical, and `base.csv` holds them at 6 decimals. If a normal draw ever differs between machines in the last bit, the comparison classes and the threshold literals' values would differ there too; nothing of the kind was reported. A rounding of the taxonomy's scalars would be a taxonomy change with new golden hashes, which this stage did not make.
2. **Numeric threshold points are not rounded.** `low + fraction * (high - low)` is computed, not drawn, and the corpus computes the same expression for the language's cut (WM.35); under the default range the points are exact. A world with a range such as `[0, 0.3]` would carry points like `0.22499999999999998` in its definition, and a fused multiply-add could in principle move the last bit between machines. Rounding the point would need the corpus's cut rounded the same way.
3. **The key-lock and comparison settings of the place constraints** are borrowed from `event_types.binary` (choice 4). If a study needs them apart for places, `place_event_types.key_lock_pairs` and `place_event_types.comparison` would be two more keys.
4. **The tiny world's place event types read the agent alone** (seed 1): both requirements drew the agent family, so no tiny requirement reads a place feature. The fast-tier tests cover a requirement over the place's features through the default world's place event types (seed 1 draws a cross constraint and two key-lock constraints) in the slow tier, and through the brute-force and agreement tests over every place literal of the tiny fixtures (the determined place feature's rule). A tiny configuration with `constraint_families: {patient: 1, cross: 1}` would guarantee a place literal in the tiny world; it was not changed, so that the tiny world stays the default settings' world.
5. **The level rule of the relation proportions** (choice 12) pairs the entity leaves with the place leaves when the entity tree is deeper. The alternative, rows only at levels both trees have, would leave the entity leaves without rows, which the corpus's capacity facts about places (stage c5b) may want.

## Notes for stage c2a

What the builder of the pair fluents (the definition, the runtime, and the fixtures) must know:

1. **The definition has four scopes now**: `entity`, `binding`, `place`, and `place_binding`, each with its own `RuleMatrices` and its own literal-value builder on `Definition` (`entity_literal_values`, `binding_literal_values`, `place_literal_values`, `place_binding_literal_values`) and on the runtime (`static_facts`, `_binding_literals`, `place_facts`, `_place_binding_literals`). The literal table is shared; `_is_place_literal` tells a place literal from an entity literal by its label, and `_literal_sort_key` puts the place groups after the entity groups. Pair literals (`pair`, `pair_some`, `pair_someof`, `pair_same`) will join the literal table with new kinds; they are computed before the matrices, like thresholds, and their values depend on the state, like fluent literals, so they belong to the entity scope (a derived fluent's rule) and to the binding scopes (preconditions and conditions read them per binding, outside the matrices, as `_precondition_holds` reads fluent literals).
2. **Places are indexed apart from entities** in `RuntimeDefinition` (`place_labels`, `place_leaves`, `place_free_values`, `place_scalar_values`, `place_count`, `place_index`), and a place event type's binding is `(entity index, place index)`; `_bindings_array` validates the two ranges. A location fluent's pairs are entity-to-place, so a `State` will need a pair part whose second index ranges over places for a location fluent and over entities for a pair fluent between entities.
3. **`EventTypeRecord.performable`** is `kind == "event_type"`; place event types have `kind == "place_event_type"` and `place == True`. Stage c3 makes them performable (their preconditions and effects come from the `move`/`act` draws); until then `apply` raises a `WorldError` for them, `able_table` skips them, and `place_able_table` holds their requirements. The `performable` lists of `episodes.py`, `fixtures.py`, and the tests read the property, so widening it is one change.
4. **The fixture format**: `places` after `entities` (only with places), the brute-force evaluator's `_row` for either kind of object, and `fixture_places(definition)` for a built definition. The format's version stays 1. Stage c2a adds `pair_fluents` in the definition, `initial_pairs` beside `initial`, and `pairs` in each step; `initial_places` waits for c3.
5. **Event files** are untouched in c1: an explicit requirement for a place event type is not supported (the loader of `event_file.py` recognizes `EVENTTYPE1` and `EVENTTYPE2` labels only). Stage c2a's event files define pair fluents and pair literals for entity event types; `place_event_types` entries (`kind: move`, a requirement over `agent.` and `patient.PLACE...` literals) come with c3.
6. **The streams** `world:places` and `world:place_event_types` are the last two of `STREAM_NAMES`; c2a adds `world:pair_fluents` and `world:pair_initial` after them (the specification's order), and the identity test of `places_off` keeps holding as long as no new draw touches an earlier stream.
7. **The comparisons** take a place scalar's argument as a place index. `value_of` decides the kind of index by the dimension, so a pair fluent's literals must not be routed through it.
8. **The corpus** reads `result.event_types` (entity event types only), `statics`, and `definition.runtime()`; it ignores `result.places`. Its `World` and the tests' `truth_oracle` build `BruteForce(record, rows)` without places, which works because they never evaluate a place literal; c4 passes `places.csv` when places enter the corpus.
