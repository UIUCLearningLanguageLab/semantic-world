### Reading of the scope

- "**Comparison classes computed as the corpus computes them now**" is read as the mean and the population standard deviation (NumPy's default) of the scalar over the instances below a category, for every category of the tree and for `ENTITY` (every entity), one row per category and scalar. The corpus keeps its own computation in this stage, and a test checks that the two agree on every row.
- "**`derive` gains `greater`, `above`, and `below`, evaluated on demand**" is read as functions of the runtime module that take entities (by index) or category means, judged by direct arithmetic on the entities' values: over the static scalars for `SCALARDIM.<n>`, and over a state's values for `NUMFL.<n>`. A category's members are the entities whose leaf is the category or below it, which the runtime reads off the leaf labels (`CATEGORY.1.3.2` is below `CATEGORY.1.3`), so no tree file is needed.
- "**The order within a step**" is read literally: `clamp(clamp(v + e) + d)` in float64, the one effect first, then the drift, each clamped to the range. `apply` applies the drift to every entity of the state (every participant of an episode is in the state, and the others are never read), and `replay` recomputes the values with the same operations in the same order, so that a recorded value is reproduced exactly.
- "**A share of effects is numeric**" and "**a share of precondition literals is numeric**" are read so that the Boolean side keeps the world's promise when the shares are 0: a Boolean effect or literal is drawn as before, from `world:effects` or `world:preconditions`, and then, from a numeric stream of its own, replaced by a numeric one at the share. With the share at 0 nothing is drawn from the numeric stream, so the Boolean effects, literals, and every later draw are those of the reference.
- "**Histories record every numeric change with its cause**" is read as: an event's `changes` list holds the numeric changes its effects made (`from`, `to`, and `cause`, the event's label), judged after the effect and before the drift; and a step records the drift's changes apart, under `drift`, each with `"cause": "drift"`, so that every numeric change of a step is recorded with its cause. A step without drift changes has no `drift` key, so the histories of a world without numeric fluents are unchanged.
- "**Numeric fluents get no words**": the corpus's fluents are the Boolean fluents of the definition (`base_fluents` and `derived_fluents` stay Boolean), so no state, change, or causal sentence can name a numeric fluent; a numeric effect is no entry for a causal statement; a derived Boolean fluent that reads a numeric threshold is judged on the replayed states, which carry the numeric values.

## Engineering choices made while building

Numbered for `docs/DECISIONS.md` (WM.E191 and following). Each is Claude Code's choice inside the stage's scope.

1. **The numeric fluent in the definition.** `NUMFL.<n>` is a symbol of kind `numeric_fluent`, carrying its `range` (`[low, high]`), its `initial` distribution (`{kind: uniform}`, `{kind: fixed, value}`, or `{kind: beta, mean, concentration}`), and its `drift` (a float, 0 for none), all hashed into the rule-set identity, because the drift is part of what the world does at every step, as an effect is. Its initial values are columns of `entities.csv`, written at full precision (polars writes the shortest round-trip form). A new module `numeric.py` holds the fluents (`NumericFluent`, `NumericFluents`, `InitialDistribution`), the draws, and the reference arithmetic (`clamp`, `step_value`). The definition and fixture format versions stay 1: every new key is absent from a world without numeric fluents.
2. **The records.** A numeric effect is `{role, fluent, operator (+= or -=), amount (above 0), expression}` with no `value`; a numeric precondition literal is `{role, fluent, operator (> or <=), threshold}` with no `value`; a numeric condition literal is `{role, kind: numeric, symbol, operator, threshold}`. A Boolean record is written exactly as before, so a reader tells the kinds apart by their keys (`amount`, `threshold`), as the fixture README says. The texts are `agent.NUMFL.1 += 0.3`, `agent.NUMFL.1 > 0.75`, and `agent.NUMFL.1 <= 0.75`, with the float in Python's shortest round-trip form (`repr`); the definition's own floats keep their 17 significant digits. In code, `Effect.value` is the signed constant of a numeric effect, `Literal.value` and `ConditionLiteral.value` are True for `>` and False for `<=`, and a `threshold` field tells a numeric literal apart; `Effect.numeric`, `Literal.numeric`, and `ConditionLiteral.kind == "numeric"` are the tests. The corpus never compares a numeric effect's value with a Boolean one (`unconditional_effects` leaves numeric effects out), so `1.0 == True` can mislead nothing.
3. **Streams of their own, by replacement.** Five streams after `world:conditions`: `world:numeric` (which fluents drift, and by how much: one draw against the share and one for the amount per fluent), `world:numeric_initial` (the initial values), `world:numeric_effects`, `world:numeric_preconditions`, and `world:numeric_conditions`. A Boolean effect or precondition literal is drawn as before, from `world:effects` or `world:preconditions`, and then replaced by a numeric one of the same role at `numeric_share`, from the numeric stream; with the share at 0 nothing is drawn from the numeric stream. So with `numeric_fluents.count: 0` the tiny and default definitions are byte-identical to the stage b1 reference (checked: `definition.json`, `entities.csv`, and every derived file but the new `comparison_classes.csv` equal the reference; `config.yaml`, the manifest's new row, and the new zero counts of `world_stats.yaml` differ), and with the numeric shares at 0, `condition_kinds.numeric` at 0, and `rule_weight` at 0, every Boolean draw is the draw of the world without numeric fluents: the rules, the effects, the preconditions, the conditions, the literal table, and the event types are equal, and only the numeric symbols and columns differ (`test_with_the_shares_at_zero_the_boolean_side_is_drawn_as_before`). The replacement, rather than a draw of the kind before the Boolean draw, keeps the Boolean effects of a world with numeric fluents a subset of the world's without them, and so keeps the two comparable. A world with numeric fluents and no Boolean fluents draws numeric effects and literals outright.
4. **Numeric condition literals** are the kind `numeric` of `event_types.effects.condition_kinds`, with the default weights `{feature: 0.4, fluent: 0.4, numeric: 0.2}` in place of stage b1's `{feature: 0.5, fluent: 0.5}`, so that the default world has numeric conditions (open question 1 below). The kind is drawn by the weights over the kinds that have symbols and a positive weight, and the fluent, side, and threshold from `world:numeric_conditions`. The even split of stage b1 (`rng.integers(2)`) is kept whenever features and fluents alone carry equal weight (the numeric weight 0, or no numeric fluents), so the Boolean conditions of a world are those of stage b1 under those settings; `condition_kinds: {feature: 4, fluent: 4, numeric: 2}` gives the default world.
5. **Derived rules over numeric thresholds.** A numeric fluent joins the input pool of a derived Boolean fluent's rule with the weight `numeric_fluents.rule_weight` (default 1, the weight of a Boolean fluent; 0 keeps them out), and a chosen one becomes a literal `NUMFL.<n> > t` at a point drawn from `event_types.preconditions.numeric_thresholds` (the same fractions as the preconditions use), a literal of kind `numeric_threshold` in the literal table, entity scope, sorted after the fluent literals. The rules are drawn from `world:fluents` as before, so with numeric fluents present the pool is larger and the Boolean rules of a world may differ from the same world's without them (the default world's two derived fluents' rules did change: see "The default world with numeric fluents"); with `count: 0` the pool and the rules are unchanged. The cone of such a derived fluent holds the numeric fluent, so a precondition or a condition on the derived fluent reads the numeric fluent for non-interference.
6. **Achievability and the rules of numeric literals.** A numeric threshold literal (in a precondition or a condition) is achievable when, for `> t`, some entity's initial value is above `t`, the fluent drifts upward, or some effect adds to it, and, for `<= t`, some initial value is at or below `t`, the fluent drifts downward, or some effect subtracts; a threshold at or outside the ends of the range is never a literal. A numeric literal is drawn again until achievable (`fix_ups.numeric_literals_redrawn` counts the rejected draws), and the Boolean literal stays when none is found. The precondition fix-ups and `check_dynamics` judge numeric literals the same way. A numeric condition literal never names a numeric fluent of a role that the precondition names (the symmetry of the Boolean rule, and the precondition draws avoid such fluents too), but may name the numeric fluent the effect itself adds to or subtracts from ("when low, add"), because unlike a Boolean condition on the effect's own fluent it makes the effect conditional in fact. A numeric effect is never empty (it can always add or subtract, clamping aside), and two effects on one numeric fluent of one role are a contradiction, as for Boolean effects.
7. **The runtime.** A `State` holds a float64 matrix of every entity's numeric fluents beside the Boolean matrix (an empty second axis in a world without them). `apply` judges numeric literals and conditions on it, adds each fired numeric effect's constant clamped to the range, and then adds every numeric fluent's drift to every entity of the state, clamped: `clamp(clamp(v + e) + d)`, the Python arithmetic the fixtures' README spells out operation by operation for the Rust runtime (the drift reaches every entity of the state, and only the participants are read; a history records the participants' changes). `apply_step` returns the values after the effects and before the drift beside the state, which the histories record, and `apply(..., drift=False)` applies the effects alone, the device by which a step's events are applied one at a time in the order-independence tests: without it, a sequential application would add the drift once per event and could make a later event illegal. A numeric effect writes its fluent and a numeric literal reads it for non-interference, through the cones of derived fluents too; the error message of an interference through a numeric read is the message of stage b1.
8. **The comparisons** are functions of `runtime.py`: `greater(definition, dimension, x, y, state)`, `above` and `below` (`definition, dimension, x, standard, z, state`), `increase` and `decrease` (`definition, fluent, x, before, after`), over entities by index or `Mean(category)`, with `ENTITY` for every entity and `RANGE` as the standard of a numeric fluent; `comparison_classes(definition)` computes every category's count, mean, and population standard deviation from the definition alone and caches it: a category's members are the entities whose leaf label is the category or below it, which the labels give (`CATEGORY.1.3.2` is below `CATEGORY.1.3`), so the runtime needs no tree file. `GREATER` is strict; `ABOVE` is at least `mean + z * sd` and `BELOW` at most `mean - z * sd`, with nothing above or below a class with no spread, as the corpus judges a pole (`Truth.pole_mask`); on a numeric fluent `ABOVE` and `BELOW` are at least and at most the fraction of the range, and `INCREASE` and `DECREASE` are strict.
9. **`derived/comparison_classes.csv`** (`category`, `scalar`, `instances`, `mean`, `sd`; `ENTITY` first, then the tree's categories in tree order; six decimals, as the other derived files) is written by `comparisons.py` from the taxonomy's tree, and three computations are checked against each other: the file against a recomputation from `base.csv` and `tree.csv` (to the file's six decimals; the in-memory frame to 1e-6), the runtime's from the leaf labels (exactly), and the corpus's `Truth.pole_mask` with `THING` for `ENTITY` (exactly). The corpus keeps its own computation until stage b3.
10. **Histories.** An event's `changes` hold the numeric changes its effects made, `{entity, fluent, from, to, cause}` with `cause` the event's label, judged after the effect and before the drift; each step holds the drift's changes to the participants under `drift`, with `"cause": "drift"`; and a history holds `initial_numeric` after `initial`, each participant's values at `TIME.1`. A step without drift changes has no `drift` key, and a world without numeric fluents writes none of the keys, so its histories are as before. `replay` recomputes every value with the runtime's arithmetic and refuses a history whose recorded values, effect or drift, differ in any bit; the JSON lines write floats in Python's shortest round-trip form. A numeric change that leaves the value where it was (clamped at the end of the range) is not recorded, as a Boolean effect that sets a value it already has is not.
11. **The brute-force evaluator and the fixtures.** The evaluator gets the numeric semantics written apart, in plain Python floats: numeric thresholds in rules, literals, and conditions; `step(state, numeric, events)`, which returns the Boolean state, the numeric values, and the values after the effects; and `compare` for a fixture's comparison entries. A fixture gains `initial_numeric`, a `numeric` record after each step, and a `comparisons` list (`kind`, `dimension`, `x`, `y`, `class`, `z`, `time`, `expected`), each absent from a world without them. Ten hand fixtures (`hand_18` to `hand_27`: three comparison cases, seven numeric cases) over a hand world with two numeric fluents (one drifting by a quarter per step) and three event types (`agent.NUMFL.1 += 0.5`; `agent.NUMFL.1 > 0.5` then `agent.NUMFL.2 -= 0.5`; `when agent.NUMFL.1 > 0.5 then agent.BOOLFL.2 := 1`), with amounts that are multiples of a quarter, so that every expected value is exact in float64 and typed by hand; the seventeen older cases gain their numeric values (every entity at 0, the drift alone). The tiny fixtures are regenerated with numeric fluents and still regenerate byte for byte.
12. **The statistics.** `world_stats.yaml` gains a `numeric_fluents` block (the count, the drifting fluents, each fluent's range, initial distribution, and drift, the numeric effects by operator, kind of event type, role, and from features, the numeric precondition literals by side, kind, role, and from features, the numeric condition literals by side and role, and the derived rules that read a threshold), `fix_ups.numeric_literals_redrawn`, `effects.condition_literals.by_symbol.numeric`, and, under `episodes`, `numeric_changes_per_event` and `drift_changes_per_step`; `mean_changes_per_event` keeps counting Boolean changes alone, so its meaning does not move. The enabling graph counts a numeric effect that adds as enabling a literal `>`, and one that subtracts a literal `<=`. The corpus's `stats.yaml` gains the same two episode figures under `scenes`.
13. **Event files** name numeric fluents under `fluents` as `NUMFL.<n>: {initial, drift}` (either optional; `drift: null` for none), write numeric precondition literals as `agent.NUMFL.1 > 0.75` or `<= 0.75` (`NOT agent.NUMFL.1 > 0.75` too, by the expression parser's `Gt`), and numeric effects as `agent.NUMFL.1 += 0.3` or `-= 0.3`, under conditions as well; the file's numeric entries are validated like the sampled ones (an unknown fluent, a constant not above 0, a threshold not strictly inside the range, a fluent twice, an unachievable literal, a condition on a fluent the precondition fixes), with errors that name the file and the entry.
14. **Episodes, the command line, and views.** `scene.initial: redraw` draws each participant's numeric fluents from their initial distributions after its Boolean fluents, participant by participant, from the episode's own part of the stream; the `define` line reports the numeric count (`2 numeric fluents`); `view --include NUMFL` adds the initial numeric columns, as `BOOLFL` adds the Boolean ones. `fluents: {count: 0}` no longer gives a world without dynamics on its own: a world without change needs `numeric_fluents: {count: 0}` too (the guide, the recipe, and the test say so; the corpus tests' `static` and `plain` worlds, and its written `deep` and `still` worlds, set it, because they test the static side and the Boolean dynamics, which the numeric fluents would reshuffle).
15. **The corpus** says nothing about numeric fluents in this stage: its fluents (`World.base_fluents`, `fluents`) stay the Boolean fluents of the definition, so no state, change, or causal sentence can name a numeric fluent, and no lexeme is made for one (the lexicon is unchanged, so the word forms are not remade); `propositions.unconditional_effects` leaves numeric effects out beside conditional ones, so no causal statement is true by a numeric effect, and the oracle's `entries` leaves out an effect with an `amount` and a literal with a `threshold`; result sentences skip numeric changes; `Truth.made_by` applies an event's recorded numeric changes (the values after the effect) beside its Boolean ones when it judges whether the event's own changes bring a derived fluent to its new value; `states_of` replays with the numeric values, and the oracle replays with the brute-force evaluator's `step`, so that a derived Boolean fluent that reads a numeric threshold is judged on the right values in state and change sentences and in the state sets (`tests/corpus/test_corpus_numeric.py`, over a tiny world with every numeric feature turned up).
16. **Figures, baselines, and the runs.** `figures.py` gains the off setting `numeric_fluents_off` (`numeric_fluents: {count: 0}`), whose baselines are the stage b1 identities of the tiny and default worlds (`6777fdc1b604` and `32356b8c6be9`), and `world_figures` gains a `numeric` block; the baselines of the other off settings move with the worlds (every tiny and default world now has numeric fluents), and the tiny corpus's hash without conditional effects moves with them. The regenerated runs go to the default folders, and the old folders are moved aside with the suffix `_pre_b2`, as the earlier stages did; the word forms are not remade, because the lexicon is unchanged, and `render` attaches the a8 word-form runs.

## Notes for stage b3

What the builder of the corpus's comparatives, numeric fluents, and conditional causal statements must know about where things stand.

- **The comparisons are in the runtime**: `greater`, `above`, `below`, `increase`, and `decrease` in `runtime.py`, over entities by index or `Mean(category)`, with `ENTITY` and `RANGE`; `comparison_classes(definition)` holds every class. The corpus's `Truth.pole_mask` and `_class_scalar` still compute the classes themselves, with `THING` for `ENTITY`; moving them to the runtime's classes (or the file `derived/comparison_classes.csv`, read through `load_derived_csv` with the identity check) is stage b3's, and `test_the_corpus_computes_the_same_classes` is the check that nothing moves. The corpus's `World` has `scalar_column`, `below`, and `pole_parts`; a comparative needs `greater` over the corpus's instance indices, which are the runtime's.
- **Numeric fluents in the corpus** need words (two absolute adjectives per fluent, `NUMFL.<n>.HIGH` and `.LOW`, with the cuts `numeric_adjectives.cut_high` and `cut_low`): `World.fluents` is Boolean, and the lexicon, `Facts.fluents`, the planner's state and result sentences, and the state test sets read it; `RuntimeDefinition.numeric_fluents`, `numeric_ranges`, `numeric_initial`, and `numeric_drift` are the data. A state at a time point comes from `Truth.states_of(scene)[time - 1].numeric`; the oracle's `numerics(scene)` is its twin. `HOLDS(..., ABOVE(NUMFL.1, REF.1, RANGE, 0.75))`, `INCREASE`, and `GREATER` at a time need new predicate kinds in `propositions.py` and new JSON forms; `interpret` must read them back.
- **Result sentences with numeric changes**: an event's numeric changes are in `HistoryEvent.changes` (`Change.numeric`, `from_`, `to`, `cause`), judged after the effect and before the drift; a change by the drift alone is in `Step.drift` and is never a result sentence. `Planner._result` skips numeric changes today (`change.numeric`), and `Truth.made_by` applies them when it judges a derived fluent.
- **`RAISES` and `LOWERS`** are true exactly when the definition has the numeric effect: `EventTypeRecord.effects` with `effect.numeric` and the sign of `effect.amount`; `unconditional_effects` leaves them out, so they need a path of their own in `has_entry` and `_evaluate_causal`, and the oracle's `entries` leaves out an effect with an `amount`. A numeric effect under a condition is, like a Boolean one, no entry until the conditional causal statements come.
- **Conditional causal statements**: `Effect.condition` holds `ConditionLiteral`s with `kind` `feature`, `fluent`, or `numeric` (the last with `operator` and `threshold`); a numeric condition is realized by the numeric adjective at the language's cut only when the threshold is the cut, so the planner must choose the statable ones. `fired_effects(definition, state, event)` says what an event wrote in a state.
- **The test worlds of the corpus**: `tiny` and `default` have numeric fluents at the default settings; `deep`, `still`, `static`, and `plain` (`tests/corpus/corpus_support.py`) have none, and `tests/corpus/test_corpus_numeric.py` builds a tiny world with every numeric feature turned up (`rule_weight: 50`, the shares at 0.6, every effect conditional with numeric conditions weighed 2), which stage b3's tests of numeric sentences can reuse.
- **The reference runs** of this stage are in `/Users/jon/Documents/Projects/semantic-world-reference/b2/` (the tiny and default worlds and corpora from `main` at 19c5cb8, before the stage).
